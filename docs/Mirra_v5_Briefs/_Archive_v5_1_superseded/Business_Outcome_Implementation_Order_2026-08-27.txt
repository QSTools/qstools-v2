# 1. PURPOSE

This sequences the remaining Business Outcome work into build order, so each
step is done once, on a stable base, without rework. Steps are ordered so
nothing gets wired downstream before the data underneath it is trustworthy.

Working method for every step below:
- PowerShell paste-and-run only (no manual file access on your end)
- Targeted in-place patches by default (`.Replace()` + `Set-Content`)
- Full-file rewrite only when a targeted patch genuinely isn't possible
- `npm run build` after every step before moving to the next
- git commit after every step, one step = one commit
- stop and check in with you after each step, not just at the end

---

# 2. WHY THIS ORDER

Labour recovery (Section 7 of the amendment) is already built, working, and
committed - that is not in this sequence.

Everything left falls into two kinds:
- fixing/hardening what Business Outcome already shows (Steps 1-2)
- wiring Business Outcome out to the modules that are supposed to consume it
  (Steps 3-5)

Wiring a downstream consumer to data that's still flagged "not yet ready for
downstream use" means redoing that wiring later. So the reconciliation
mismatch gets resolved before any downstream wiring starts.

# 3. STEP ORDER

## STEP 1 - Housekeeping check (near-zero cost, do first)

Confirm the previous session's loose ends are actually closed:
- confirm diagnostic console.log lines are out of
  hooks/useBusinessOutcomeLabourRecovery.js
- confirm git status is clean (only your own two known untouched files should
  ever show as modified)

Output: confirmation only, patch if the diagnostic lines are still there.

## STEP 2 - Resolve the "Reconciliation mismatch" warning

The top banner on /business-outcome currently reads "Partial - some upstream
sources not yet ready" with a "Reconciliation mismatch" flag, sourced from
hooks/useBusinessOutcomeTruth.js. This is the Stage 2 truth-chain check
comparing Business Summary against Revenue/COG truth.

This needs a short investigation before anything else, because:
- it's the thing actually gating "downstream_ready"
- we don't yet know if it's a real data problem or a stale/overly strict check

Action: inspect useBusinessOutcomeTruth.js's reconciliation logic and the
actual current values it's comparing, identify whether it's a genuine
mismatch (real data problem, do not silently patch) or a check that needs
correcting. Report back before patching.

## STEP 3 - Formalise the output_contract in code

Right now labour_recovery is returned by its own hook with its own shape.
The v5.0/v5.1 brief specifies a single output_contract object (Section 6 of
v5.0 + Section 5 of the v5.1 amendment). Before wiring downstream consumers,
Business Outcome's page-level data should be assembled into that one
documented shape, so every downstream consumer reads the same contract
instead of reaching into multiple hooks individually.

Action: add a thin selector (e.g. businessOutcomeOutputContractSelectors.js)
that composes useBusinessOutcomeTruth + useBusinessOutcomeLabourRecovery into
the documented output_contract shape. Existing hooks and the page's own
rendering are not touched - this is additive, for downstream use only.

## STEP 4 - Wire AI-readable Business State Export (S06)

lib/selectors/aiBusinessStateSelectors.js currently packages business_summary,
business_modelling, and selected_model. It does not include business_outcome.

Action: add a business_outcome section to the export object, sourced from the
Step 3 output_contract selector. Read-only inclusion, no recalculation, per
S06's own locked boundaries.

This is lower-risk than Dashboard or Business Modelling because /ai-business-
state is developer/admin-facing, not customer-facing - a good first downstream
consumer to prove the contract works before touching user-facing pages.

## STEP 5 - Wire Business Overview Dashboard (S07) and/or Business Modelling (S05)

Once Step 4 confirms the contract holds up, decide together whether Dashboard,
Business Modelling, or both are worth wiring now versus deferring. Both are
"display/consume only, don't recalculate" per their own locked boundaries, but
they're user-facing, so worth doing deliberately rather than both at once.

## STEP 6 - Revenue-split visual ("where every revenue dollar goes")

Earlier in this build (before Steps 1-5), a visual treatment for /business-
outcome was discussed and mocked up: a horizontal bar showing revenue split
by percentage across Labour / Assets / Overheads / COG / Net profit, instead
of (or alongside) the current list-style figures. The intent was for this to
be the page's real payoff - showing the shape of the business at a glance,
not just confirming it's viable.

This was agreed in principle but never built, and was not part of Steps 1-5.
It sits here, after downstream wiring, because:
- it's a presentation layer on top of data Steps 1-3 already made correct
  (total_revenue, total_cost_burden broken into labour/asset/overhead,
  total_COG) - building it before Step 2's reconciliation fix would have
  meant visualising numbers that were still wrong
- it doesn't block AI Export, Dashboard, or Business Modelling consuming the
  output_contract, so it doesn't need to happen before Step 4/5

Action: confirmed during investigation - Business Summary's
cost_burden_breakdown (the field originally assumed to hold this split) is
dead code, always resolving to its own empty default at every point in the
chain. See KNOWN_ISSUE_cost_burden_breakdown_dead_field_v5_1.txt for the
full trace. Not fixed now - out of scope for this step - but logged so it
isn't lost.

The visual instead uses the real, separately-populated fields already in
Business Summary's output_contract: total_people_cost_annual (labour),
total_asset_cost_annual (assets), total_business_overheads (overheads),
total_direct_costs (COG, via the truth-chain's total_COG), total_revenue,
and net_position (net profit). Build the bar visual as a new component on
the existing /business-outcome page. Read-only, same as the labour recovery
card - no new calculation, only presentation of numbers already in the
output_contract.
