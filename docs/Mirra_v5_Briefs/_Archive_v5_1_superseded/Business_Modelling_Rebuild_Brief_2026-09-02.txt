# 2. CORE PRINCIPLE — REVENUE IS NEVER A USER INPUT

This is the single most important rule in this brief and must not be
relaxed by any future session without an explicit, separate conversation.

The user must never be able to type in a target revenue number and have
the model work backward to make the business look viable. Revenue only
ever appears as a READ-OUT — the consequence of a lever (a rate change,
later a utilisation change) — never as a free-typed input.

The one designed exception is the breakeven toggle (Section 4), which
computes "what would total revenue have needed to be, at today's cost
structure and mix, to reach zero net profit." This is a diagnostic
question the system answers FOR the user, not a field the user fills in.
It is not a lever, and it does not let the user set an arbitrary number —
it is a single derived figure with one correct answer given the current
state.

---

# 3. SCOPE — SMOOTHED MODE ONLY

This entire feature (breakeven toggle, group rate levers, isolated
per-group breakeven) operates ONLY in Real Capacity's smoothed mode
("How the Business Runs"), never naive mode ("Each Part On Its Own") and
never Assumed Capacity.

Reasoning, confirmed with user: breakeven and the per-group lever both
depend on walking the smoothed cascade's shortfall-sharing hierarchy —
which group absorbs how much of a shortfall based on what it can actually
afford to give up. That hierarchy does not exist in naive mode (nothing is
"carried" in naive mode by definition — see S24) or in Assumed Capacity
(flat percentage split, no hierarchy to walk). If a future session wants
an Assumed Capacity or naive equivalent of this feature, that is a new
design conversation, not an assumed extension of this brief.

# 4. FEATURE 1 — ACTUAL / BREAKEVEN TOGGLE ON A REUSED CARD 1

## 4.1 What gets reused

Business Modelling reuses Business Outcome's Card 1 shape (headline net
profit figure + attention list of sources) as-is — same visual format,
same familiar territory for the user. This is a new card built for
Business Modelling, not a live import of the Business Outcome component,
but it must look and behave the same way so the user recognises it
immediately.

## 4.2 The toggle

Two states:

- ACTUAL — today's real smoothed net profit and attention list, exactly
  as Business Outcome Card 1 currently shows it.
- BREAKEVEN — a hypothetical view showing what every source's numbers
  would look like if total revenue were exactly the amount required to
  bring smoothed net profit to zero, holding today's cost structure and
  rate/cost mix constant, run through the same cascade logic used
  everywhere else.

## 4.3 How breakeven is calculated

Breakeven is NOT a separate calculation bolted on top of the cascade. It
runs the same cascade machinery in reverse: solve for the single revenue
level that makes the cascade's net profit output equal zero, then run
that revenue level forward through the normal cascade to produce every
source's resulting figures.

One cascade, two directions:
- forward: actual inputs → net profit (existing, already built)
- backward: target net profit (zero) → required revenue → forward through
  cascade → per-source figures (new, this brief)

## 4.4 What breakeven mode implies for the attention list

Under breakeven, the attention list shows every source's figures AS THEY
WOULD BE at that hypothetical revenue level, not today's real figures
sitting under a hypothetical headline. The whole card re-renders as one
coherent hypothetical state, sourced from one backward-solved revenue
number pushed through the existing forward cascade.

# 5. FEATURE 2 — PER-OPERATING-GROUP RATE LEVER

## 5.1 What a lever is, in this phase

Phase 1 supports exactly one lever type: a blended rate adjustment per
operating group. The user can raise or lower a group's rate (e.g. "+$5/hr
on Digger + Operator") and see that group's — and via the cascade, the
whole business's — resulting net profit.

Confirmed constraints for this phase:

- Hours are held constant. A rate change only moves revenue by
  rate x existing hours — it never assumes more hours get sold.
- One blended rate per group. Groups that mix labour and asset
  components (e.g. digger + operator combining asset recovery and labour
  recovery into one sell rate) are NOT split into separate levers for
  their underlying components in this phase. The extra/reduced amount
  from a rate change is not attributed to asset vs labour internally —
  it doesn't need to be, because the cascade only consumes the group's
  total resulting revenue and cost, not its internal composition.
  Splitting the rate into its asset/labour components is a possible
  future refinement (shows the user "how much of your increase is asset
  recovery vs labour recovery") but is explicitly out of scope for phase
  1.

## 5.2 Three reference points per group

Each operating group's rate row shows three numbers on the same line:

1. CURRENT RATE — today's actual blended rate for that group.
2. ISOLATED BREAKEVEN RATE — what this group's rate alone would need to
   become, if this were the ONLY lever pulled and every other group's
   rate stayed exactly as it is today, for the WHOLE BUSINESS's smoothed
   net profit to reach zero.
3. USER TARGET RATE — a value the user types in. May sit below, at, or
   above the isolated breakeven rate.

## 5.3 The circular-reference problem and how it's resolved

Per-group isolated breakeven numbers cannot all be true simultaneously if
the user is imagining changing multiple groups' rates at once — moving
one group's rate changes how much shortfall the cascade shares onto every
other group, which would change their breakeven numbers too. Solving all
groups jointly is a genuinely different (and confusing) feature, and is
explicitly rejected for this phase.

Resolution (confirmed with user): each group's isolated breakeven number
is a SNAPSHOT, not a locked/persistent target. It is recalculated fresh,
based on the current state of every other group, every time the page
(or any lever) changes. It always answers "given everything else exactly
as it currently sits, what would this one group alone need to do" — never
"what would it need to do if other things also changed." This must be
stated in the UI or its supporting copy so the number is never
misread as a fixed, standalone truth.

## 5.4 Below-breakeven state

If the user's target rate sits below the isolated breakeven rate for that
group, the UI must visibly flag this as a still-a-loss state (exact
visual treatment TBD at build time, matching existing pill/status
conventions per S17 Section 16 style — do not invent new colour/status
language ad hoc). Above breakeven should read as a surplus/profit-above-
target state, not merely "resolved."

# 6. EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE FOR THIS PHASE — DEFERRED

- **Utilisation lever.** Architecturally compatible with the cascade (the
  cascade doesn't care whether a group's revenue/cost changed because of
  a rate move or a utilisation move — it just consumes the resulting
  group-level revenue and cost). NOT trivial to build, because utilisation
  changes both revenue (more billable hours) AND cost recovery per hour
  (fixed asset ownership cost spread over more hours) — a real
  second-order effect requiring Assets' own utilisation/recovery-basis
  data. Treat as a distinct future lever type, not an extension of the
  rate lever's math.
- Splitting a group's blended rate into separate asset/labour rate levers.
- Naive-mode or Assumed-Capacity equivalents of breakeven or the group
  lever.
- Multi-group simultaneous/joint breakeven solving.
- Whether a user's target-rate scenario persists (saved) across sessions,
  or is a live sandbox that resets on page load. NOT YET DECIDED — needs
  an explicit answer before build, flagged in Section 8.
- Labour productivity, overhead, and combined-scenario levers from the
  v4.0 brief (Sections 20-23) — real, still-valid future work, not part
  of this phase.

# 7. LOCKED BOUNDARIES (carried forward from S05, unchanged)

Business Modelling must not:

- overwrite Labour
- overwrite Assets
- overwrite General Overheads
- overwrite Cost Summary
- overwrite Revenue Summary
- overwrite Business Summary
- overwrite Business Outcome
- override Model Readiness
- infer missing source truth
- patch missing values
- change source module calculations

Business Modelling may only:

- read source output contracts, including Business Outcome's Real
  Capacity output (group_real_capacity, naive_net_profit, final_net_profit)
- read Model Readiness
- store local scenario/lever assumptions
- calculate modelling-only gaps, breakeven revenue, and isolated per-group
  breakeven rates
- expose selected model output
- expose downstream readiness flags

# 10. FINAL PRINCIPLE

Business Modelling shows what happens if the business changes how it
earns — never what happens if the business simply earns more. Every
number this page shows the user must be traceable back to a lever they
could actually pull, or a diagnostic question the system answered for
them — never a number they were allowed to assume into existence.
