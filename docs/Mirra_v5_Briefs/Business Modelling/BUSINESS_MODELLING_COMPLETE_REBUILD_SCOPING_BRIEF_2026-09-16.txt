MIRRA v5.2 - BUSINESS MODELLING COMPLETE REBUILD - SCOPING BRIEF
STATUS: DRAFT - FOR REVIEW BEFORE ANY CODE CHANGES
DATE: 2026-09-16
ORIGIN: Surfaced while live-testing the materials/View A-B independence
fix (same session). Fixing materials revealed a deeper, pre-existing
problem: Business Modelling has never actually shown the business's
real, current, reconciled state - only an independent-lever view that
happened to sum close to reality because materials was silently acting
as a residual plug. Removing that plug (today's fix) didn't create the
problem, it just stopped masking it.

===============================================================================
1. THE ACTUAL PROBLEM, STATED PLAINLY
===============================================================================

Every function in businessModellingIndependentCalculations.js was
deliberately built (S31 brief) to judge each source "against ONLY its own
true_cost... never a business-wide total." That is correct and useful
FOR MODELLING A FIX - "if this source stood entirely on its own, what
would it need to charge to break even?"

It is NOT a correct representation of "how is the business actually
doing right now" - that requires the real, cascade-adjusted, reconciled
figures (cross-subsidy included), which is what Business Outcome's
headline_real_capacity already shows and is already trusted.

Business Modelling's page has always opened directly on the independent
view, headlined as "Your business is currently making $X in net profit"
- a real-sounding claim built from unreal, non-reconciled numbers. Before
today, this was disguised by Materials silently absorbing whatever gap
existed (a residual, not an independent figure), so the total happened
to land close to the real number by construction, not by honesty. Today's
materials fix (session same date) removed that disguise - the business
was at real $0 breakeven, and the independent-view total came out at
$767,271 - the size of the cross-subsidy currently hidden in the rate
structure between sources, not a real profit figure.

===============================================================================
2. THE DECISION - CONFIRMED, 2026-09-16
===============================================================================

Business Modelling is being rebuilt to genuinely separate two different
questions it currently conflates:

**Section A - Current business state (real, reconciled)**
"Here's where the business actually is right now" - matches Business
Outcome exactly. Real total net profit (including cross-subsidy), each
source's actual current rate/margin as things stand today. This is a
STARTING POINT, not a lever exercise.

**Section B - Model a fix (independent, lever-driven)**
The existing lever engine, kept, but explicitly reframed as "if each
source stood fully on its own, with no help from any other source" -
matching the same honest framing Business Outcome already uses for its
own "Each Part On Its Own" / View A-B toggle. Used for deciding WHICH
lever to pull, never presented as today's reality.

Nothing in this module is on a "leave untouched" list anymore - full
rebuild authorised, including BusinessModellingLeverCard.jsx and
businessModellingIndependentCalculations.js (both previously untouchable
per Section 7 of BUSINESS_OUTCOME_FILE_MAP_v5_1.txt - THAT status is
REVOKED for these two files specifically, by this decision. The other
three files on that list - SidebarNavigation.jsx, the S22 brief, and
generalOverheadCategorySelectors.js - are NOT part of this decision and
remain untouched).

===============================================================================
3. WHAT ALREADY EXISTS AND CAN BE REUSED (Section A's data)
===============================================================================

Section A does NOT need new calculation logic - the real, reconciled
figures already exist and are already trusted, via the same per_source
object Business Modelling already receives:

  - per_source.headline_real_capacity - real total_net_profit,
    total_modelled_revenue, all_sources (each source's real,
    cascade-adjusted net profit and verdict) - this IS Business
    Outcome's own trusted headline, already flowing into
    useBusinessModelling() today.
  - per_source.real_capacity.group_real_capacity[] - per-group real
    current rate (modelled_revenue / group_recovery_hours), for showing
    "your rates and margins as they are today."
  - per_source.materials (View A residual - NOT to be used here) vs
    per_source.view_b.materials (real, independently-anchored - the
    field the materials fix now reads, but for THIS section, the
    correct figure is whichever one the real reconciled total already
    uses - needs confirming which, see Section 5 below).

This is "reuse the engine, don't rebuild it" - the exact principle this
whole module was originally built on (S05 build brief, Section 3: "must
not recalculate source modules... may only read existing output
contracts").

7. REVISED DIRECTION (2026-09-16, later same day) - REAL ENGINE RERUN,
   NOT SECTION A/B, GROUNDED IN ACTUAL REPO DISCOVERY
===============================================================================

Two external architecture briefs were reviewed this session (Copilot-authored,
no repo access - "MIRRA v5.2 Business Modelling Orchestration and Page Flow"
and "MIRRA v5.2 Business Outcome and Modelling Concept Decision Record", both
dated 2026-09-16, not saved to this repo, session-only). Their core principle
is CORRECT and now adopted: Business Modelling should not be a second
diagnostic layer (Section A/B as originally proposed in this brief, Section 2
above) - it should be Baseline (the real Business Outcome state, unchanged)
plus an ISOLATED RERUN of the same real calculation chain with user-adjusted
inputs, not a separate independent-aggregate calculation standing in for the
whole-business result.

Their SEQUENCING/EFFORT ESTIMATE (an 8-phase, multi-week extraction project)
is NOT adopted as-is - it was reasoned from zero knowledge of this actual
codebase. Real discovery this session found the situation is significantly
better than assumed:

**Cost Allocation (hooks/useCostAllocation.js, confirmed 2026-09-16):**
The entire calculation is one call: `calculate_cost_allocation(calculation_inputs)`
(lib/calculations/costAllocationRules.js), a pure function - plain object in,
plain object out. Everything else in the hook either assembles
calculation_inputs beforehand (build_cost_allocation_inputs, labour type row
builders, asset overlay, group recovery rows builder - ALL pure, take plain
data, no storage/hook access inside them) or reshapes the result afterward for
display. The hook already partially supports override -
`useCostAllocation(inputs = {})` lets recovery_summary be injected from
outside today. labour, assets, and state (from useCostAllocationStorage) are
NOT yet injectable - they always come from live hooks/storage.

**What a real rerun requires (Cost Allocation specifically):** extract the
existing assembly sequence (build_cost_allocation_inputs -> asset overlay ->
labour type rows -> group recovery rows -> calculate_cost_allocation) out of
useCostAllocation.js into one standalone function taking
{ labour_output_contract, assets_output_contract, recovery_summary,
allocation_state } and returning `calculated`. The live hook calls it with
real data (unchanged behaviour - must be parity-tested, per the Copilot
brief's own Phase 2/3 discipline, which IS being kept). Business Modelling
calls the SAME function with a modified copy of one input. This is a scoped,
single-file extraction - relocating an already-pure call sequence, not
rewriting calculation logic - not the multi-week effort originally estimated.

**Rate Builder (hooks/rate-builder/useRateBuilderLabourRates.js) - NOT YET
CHECKED.** costAllocationGroupCostBuilder.js and
rateBuilderLabourRateCalculations.js (lib/calculations/) were both confirmed
pure this session (plain params in, plain result out) - promising, but the
hook itself (the assembly/storage layer, same question as Cost Allocation
above) has not yet been read. This is the next concrete discovery step.

**Standing principle going forward:** any decision about HOW to build this
rebuild is grounded in this repo's actual code, confirmed by direct reading -
not assumed from the external briefs, which are useful for the CONCEPT
(reuse real engines, isolated state, don't duplicate calculations - all
correct and consistent with this project's founding principle) but not
authoritative on implementation specifics, sequencing, or effort, since they
were written without repo access.

**Rate Builder (hooks/rate-builder/useRateBuilderLabourRates.js, confirmed
2026-09-16):** the actual calculation, calculate_labour_rate_result()
(lib/calculations/rateBuilderLabourRateCalculations.js), is pure and
genuinely simple - five plain numbers in (labour_cost_rate,
productive_efficiency_percent, recovery_allowance_rate,
target_margin_percent, current_charge_out_rate), one result object out.
Easier to rerun in isolation than Cost Allocation's calculation, taken
alone.

BUT: unlike useCostAllocation(inputs = {}), which at least allows
recovery_summary to be overridden, useRateBuilderLabourRates() takes ZERO
parameters - it calls useCostAllocation() directly and internally,
hardcoded, with no injection point at all. This CONFIRMS (in real code,
not assumption) the dependency chain the external Copilot brief predicted:
Rate Builder cannot be isolated independently of Cost Allocation - it must
be isolated together, with Cost Allocation's isolated output fed in as
Rate Builder's input. They are not two independently-swappable pieces.

ALSO WORTH FLAGGING: Rate Builder's pricing formula (cost / efficiency +
recovery allowance, then margin-adjusted) is a GENUINELY DIFFERENT
calculation from Business Modelling's own existing independent calc
(true_cost / hours, working backward from real annual cost) - not the
same calculation in different clothes. They answer different framing
questions: "what should I charge given my cost structure and target
margin" (Rate Builder) vs. "what would this group need to earn to cover
its real cost" (Business Modelling's current independent engine). Do not
assume Business Modelling should call Rate Builder's function directly
without confirming which question the rebuilt Business Modelling is
actually meant to answer.

**Assets (hooks/useAssets.js, confirmed 2026-09-16):** calculateAssetOutputs()
(lib/calculations/assetCalculations.js) is fully pure - plain asset_state,
saved_assets, and override params in, plain result object out (confirmed
by reading the whole function, ~14KB, no storage/hook access anywhere
inside it). Same pattern as Cost Allocation and Rate Builder.

BUT, same problem as Rate Builder: useAssets() takes ZERO parameters -
no injection point at all. Worse than Rate Builder in one respect -
it is hardcoded directly to THREE upstream live hooks, not one:
useProfitAndLoss(), useGeneralOverheads(), useOpeningHours(). This widens
the real dependency graph beyond what was originally scoped (Cost
Allocation -> Rate Builder -> Operating Groups -> Business Outcome) -
Assets alone reaches further upstream into P&L, General Overheads, and
Opening Hours too.

**UNIFIED FINDING ACROSS ALL THREE MODULES CHECKED (Cost Allocation, Rate
Builder, Assets):** every actual calculation is a pure function, already
separable from live data by construction - genuinely good news, the "reuse
the engine" principle is achievable everywhere checked so far. But NO hook
is currently built to accept an isolated/modified input as a first-class
feature - Cost Allocation has one partial override field
(recovery_summary), Rate Builder and Assets have none at all. This is one
consistent architectural gap across the whole upstream chain, not three
separate problems - the real Phase 2 work (per the external Copilot
brief's own phasing, Section 27) is adding a consistent injection
mechanism to each hook, not rewriting any calculation logic, which is
already correct and reusable as-is.

**Business Outcome itself (hooks/useBusinessOutcomePerSourceRevenue.js,
confirmed 2026-09-16) - DISCOVERY PASS COMPLETE, final and largest piece:**
same pattern holds exactly, at the very end of the chain. Every calculation
function inside (build_labour_sources, build_asset_sources,
apply_revenue_ceiling, apply_real_capacity - both View A and View B
variants) is pure - plain data in, plain data out, hand-verified against a
spreadsheet and a 200,000-step numerical simulation per the file's own
comments. But the hook itself, export default function
useBusinessOutcomePerSourceRevenue(), takes ZERO parameters - the widest
fan-in of anything checked: hardcoded directly to FIVE upstream live
hooks (useBusinessOutcomeLabourRecovery, useCostAllocation,
useBusinessSummary, useOpeningHours, useAssets). Makes sense - this is
the final assembly point everything else funnels into - but it means the
same injection-point gap exists here too, at the highest-stakes layer.

**FILE SIZE FLAG (not urgent, not to be fixed as a side effect of
anything else):** useBusinessOutcomePerSourceRevenue.js is genuinely
large (1000+ lines) - it carries both View A and View B's full cascades,
the capacity coverage gap diagnostic, and the health gauge baseline all
in one file. Per Working Conventions, splitting an existing large file
is a separate, deliberate decision, not a drive-by fix - noting this as
a known future candidate, especially since the injection-point mechanism
will need to modify this exact file most.

**DISCOVERY PASS NOW COMPLETE across the full chain: Cost Allocation ->
Rate Builder -> Assets -> Business Outcome.** The unified finding above
holds at every single layer with zero exceptions: calculations are
already pure and reusable; every hook needs a real injection mechanism
added. Next real decision: design what that injection mechanism actually
looks like (a consistent inputs parameter pattern? a shared
isolated-state wrapper? per-hook or one shared approach?) - not yet
decided, deliberately not decided in the middle of discovery.

**NEW INSIGHT (2026-09-16, user's own idea, later same session):**
BusinessOutcomePerSourceRevenueCard.jsx (per BUSINESS_OUTCOME_FILE_MAP_v5_1.txt
Section 1) already receives per_source as a PROP, not derived internally
via its own hook call - meaning the entire real Outcome display (every
table, ledger, gauge, the View A/View B toggle) may already be reusable
AS-IS, unmodified, the moment Business Modelling can produce a second,
modelled per_source object to hand it instead of the real one. This

toggled or compared side by side - genuinely note-for-note, by
construction, not by manual upkeep. This also directly answers the
future dashboard/performance-comparison need flagged by the user - every
metric Outcome already computes becomes automatically available in
modelled form too, with no separate dashboard-specific calculation ever
needing to be built. NOT YET VERIFIED - the Card's actual prop signature
still needs confirming (in progress, same session) before this is locked
in as the plan.
