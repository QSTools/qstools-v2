MIRRA v5.2 - BUSINESS OUTCOME / BUSINESS MODELLING
CONCEPTUAL ARCHITECTURE AND DECISION RECORD

STATUS: CONCEPT CONFIRMED - IMPLEMENTATION NOT YET SCOPED
DATE: 2026-09-16
ORIGIN: Formal record of the discussion reviewing the Business Modelling rebuild proposal and its relationship to Business Outcome, Cost Allocation and Rate Builder.

===============================================================================
1. PURPOSE OF THIS RECORD
===============================================================================

This document formalises the agreed conceptual purpose of Business Outcome and
Business Modelling before further design or code changes are made.

The discussion began with a proposal to separate Business Modelling into a
"current business state" section and an "independent modelling" section. Further
review established that the relationship is more precise than a simple split
between reality and an independent calculation.

The confirmed concept is:

  Business Outcome = the current solved state of the business.

  Business Modelling = an isolated, adjustable second state of that same
  business, run through the same underlying calculation architecture.

Business Modelling is therefore not a separate diagnostic system, and it must
not become a second calculation truth. It is a controlled modelling layer placed
on top of the trusted Business Outcome baseline.

===============================================================================
2. THE FOUNDATIONAL BUSINESS LOGIC
===============================================================================

The business cannot be meaningfully modelled from accounting outputs alone.
Revenue, gross profit, net profit and cash are consequences of how the business
creates, prices and recovers its costs. They are outputs, not the primary
operating levers.

The modelling foundation is the operating chain already established in Mirra:

  Imported financial truth, including P&L and Balance Sheet data
                              |
                              v
                       Cost Allocation
                              |
                              v
                         Rate Builder
                              |
                              v
                       Operating Groups
                              |
                              v
                      Business Outcome

Cost Allocation establishes the real cost burden carried by the business and
assigns that cost to the relevant operating groups.

Rate Builder prices those operating groups using their allocated costs,
recoverable hours, asset assumptions, overhead contribution and other approved
source inputs.

The operating groups are the mechanisms through which the business generates
cost and revenue. Examples include labour groups, asset groups and
Materials/COGS.

Business Outcome then runs those real business inputs through the reconciled
business engine and shows the resulting current position.

Accordingly, meaningful modelling must begin with these same operating groups
and the same cost and pricing architecture. Modelling directly from top-level
P&L or Balance Sheet outputs would not explain how the business generates or
changes revenue and profit.

===============================================================================
3. BUSINESS OUTCOME - CURRENT STATE
===============================================================================

Business Outcome represents the current, reconciled state of the business based
on the information presently held in Mirra.

Its inputs are grounded in the specific business, including:

  - imported P&L information;
  - imported Balance Sheet information;
  - allocated business costs;
  - operating-group structure;
  - current charge-out rates;
  - asset costs and recovery assumptions;
  - labour costs and recovery assumptions;
  - overhead allocation and recovery;
  - assumed productive or operating hours where live data is not yet available;
  - current revenue attribution and reconciliation rules.

Business Outcome is not merely an accounting scoreboard. It already answers the
core diagnostic questions, including:

  - what the business is currently producing as an overall outcome;
  - whether the business covers its total cost;
  - how each operating group stands on its own;
  - which operating groups cover their own costs;
  - which operating groups are supported by cross-subsidy;
  - the current cost and charge-out basis for labour and assets;
  - the current overhead recovery position;
  - the business breakeven position;
  - the difference between the whole-business result and each-part-on-its-own
    views;
  - areas where source assumptions or imported financial data require further
    checking.

Business Outcome is therefore both the current-state result and the existing
business diagnosis. Business Modelling does not need to create another
independent diagnostic layer.

Important qualification:

The current state is only as complete as the evidence available. Where live
operational data is not yet connected, assumptions such as productive labour
hours or asset running hours remain explicit business inputs. These are not fake
metrics. They are the business's current stated assumptions, anchored to real
cost and financial data. Future live data, including time tracking and actual
utilisation, will test and improve those assumptions.

===============================================================================
4. BUSINESS MODELLING - ADJUSTED OR FUTURE STATE
===============================================================================

Business Modelling represents an isolated, adjustable version of the same
business shown in Business Outcome.

Its purpose is to answer:

  "If the business changes its rates, recovery or overhead position, what
  happens to the business outcome?"

Business Modelling must not invent a separate business or recalculate the
business from unrelated assumptions. It must take the trusted Business Outcome
baseline, apply controlled changes to the relevant operating levers, and push
the adjusted state back through the same Business Outcome calculation module in
isolation.

Conceptually:

  Outcome = Business Engine(Current Inputs)

  Modelling = Business Engine(Current Inputs + Selected Adjustments)

The calculation engine and output contracts remain common. The state supplied
to the engine differs.

Business Outcome remains unchanged while the user models alternatives.
Business Modelling is therefore a second layer on top of Outcome, not a mutation
of the baseline state.

The modelling state is valid because it remains grounded in:

  - the same imported financial position;
  - the same cost allocation;
  - the same operating groups;
  - the same source identities;
  - the same true-cost calculations;
  - the same revenue and recovery architecture;
  - the same reconciliation rules;
  - the same Business Outcome engine.

Only the explicitly selected modelling inputs are changed.

===============================================================================
5. CURRENT STATE AND INDEPENDENT STATE ARE INTRINSICALLY LINKED
===============================================================================

The current state and independent modelling state are not competing truths and
must not be presented as unrelated calculations.

They are the same business under two states:

  CURRENT / ACTUAL STATE
  ----------------------
  The present solved position produced by Business Outcome using current inputs.

  ADJUSTED / MODELLED STATE
  -------------------------
  The isolated position produced by running selected operating-group changes
  through the same Business Outcome engine.

The current state is the baseline for every modelled state. Business Modelling
cannot be valid until Business Outcome is correctly established because the
modelled state inherits its costs, operating structure, rates, assumptions and
financial anchors from Outcome.

Likewise, Business Modelling exists so that the user can evaluate a possible
change before deciding whether to implement it. If a change is later implemented
and real data is updated, that change eventually becomes part of a future
Business Outcome baseline.

The resulting operating loop is:

  Business Outcome: current solved state
                  |
                  v
  Identify the operating-group change to test
                  |
                  v
  Business Modelling: isolated adjusted state
                  |
                  v
  Compare current versus adjusted outcomes
                  |
                  v
  Decide whether to implement the change
                  |
                  v
  New real inputs and live results eventually update Business Outcome
                  |
                  +-----------------------------------------------+

This circular relationship is intentional. It creates a continuous process of
understanding, modelling, decision-making and later verification.

===============================================================================
6. THE REAL BUSINESS LEVERS
===============================================================================

The agreed primary modelling levers are:

  1. Labour Recovery
  2. Asset Recovery
  3. Overhead Recovery

The relevant adjustable inputs can include, where supported by the existing
business architecture:

  - labour charge-out rates;
  - labour recovery assumptions;
  - labour productive-hour assumptions;
  - asset charge-out rates;
  - asset utilisation or operating-hour assumptions;
  - asset recovery assumptions;
  - overhead cost changes;
  - overhead contribution or recovery;
  - operating-group target rates.

Revenue, gross profit, net profit and cash are not themselves the fundamental
levers. They are derived outcomes produced when the actual operating levers
change.

A user cannot meaningfully "turn up net profit" as an isolated decision. The
user can change a labour rate, asset rate, utilisation assumption, recovery
position or overhead burden, after which the business engine calculates the
resulting effects on revenue and profit.

This distinction is central to Mirra's purpose. The application is intended to
model how a business works, not merely apply arbitrary percentages to accounting
headlines.

===============================================================================
7. OPERATING GROUPS ARE THE MODELLING UNIT
===============================================================================

Business Modelling should operate in the same terms used by Cost Allocation and
Rate Builder: the operating groups.

For each applicable operating group, the system can retain and compare items
such as:

  - current rate;
  - true cost or minimum recoverable cost;
  - allocated overhead contribution;
  - full-cost breakeven rate;
  - current assumed hours or utilisation;
  - selected target rate;
  - selected target hours or utilisation;
  - current revenue and recovery;
  - adjusted revenue and recovery;
  - current contribution or net position;
  - adjusted contribution or net position.

The Foreman example already points towards this design by displaying:

  - Current rate;
  - Rate needed to break even for the group alone;
  - User-selected target rate.

That comparison is conceptually correct. It preserves the current source truth,
shows the independently required rate and allows the user to choose a modelled
rate. The modelled target must then be passed through the full isolated Outcome
state so all whole-business consequences are calculated consistently.

===============================================================================
8. BASELINE, BREAKEVEN AND MODELLED SCENARIOS
===============================================================================

The scenario concepts should be anchored as follows.

A. BASELINE

The Baseline is the current Business Outcome state.

It means:

  "Where the business stands under its current reconciled inputs and current
  assumptions."

Baseline must not be generated by an unrelated or alternate calculation path.
It must use the trusted current Outcome result.

B. BREAKEVEN / MINIMUM ACCEPTABLE POSITION

Breakeven is the point at which the business covers its real costs.

Breakeven can sit above or below the current Baseline:

  - If the current business is below breakeven, breakeven represents the minimum
    improvement required to stop losing money.
  - If the current business is above breakeven, breakeven represents the lower
    boundary or downside position the business should not fall below.

Breakeven is therefore a structural reference point, not automatically an
"upside" or "downside" in every business. Its relationship to the Baseline must
be determined from the current state.

The modelling tool may offer breakeven as a default selectable scenario because
it is a meaningful minimum operating position.

C. ADJUSTED / TARGET STATE

The adjusted state is created when the user changes one or more operating
levers. It represents the projected outcome from those selected changes.

D. UPSIDE AND DOWNSIDE

Upside and Downside scenarios must remain anchored to the real Baseline and use
the same isolated Outcome engine.

They are combinations of explicit adjusted assumptions, not independent
headline calculations.

The naming and presentation should make clear whether a scenario is:

  - the unchanged current Baseline;
  - the calculated breakeven boundary;
  - a user-defined target;
  - an upside case; or
  - a downside case.

===============================================================================
9. SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH - NON-NEGOTIABLE RULE
===============================================================================

Business Outcome and Business Modelling must never develop separate definitions
of the same business metric.

The following principle is confirmed:

  One business engine, one set of source identities, one set of calculation
  contracts, and two isolated states.

Business Modelling must reuse the same trusted modules and outputs that produce
Business Outcome. It must not independently recreate source costs, allocations,
rates, revenue logic, cross-subsidy logic or reconciliation unless a shared
calculation module is deliberately changed for both states.

If Outcome and Modelling separately calculate the same item, the system risks
creating two different businesses:

  - one represented by the baseline Outcome state; and
  - another represented by a disconnected modelling state.

That would produce fictitious or misleading outcomes even if each page appeared
internally consistent.

The correct isolation is state isolation, not logic duplication.

  Correct:
    Same engine + cloned baseline state + explicit modelling adjustments.

  Incorrect:
    Separate modelling calculations that only approximate or imitate the
    current Outcome.

===============================================================================
10. HOW CROSS-SUBSIDY AND INDEPENDENT CALCULATIONS FIT
===============================================================================

The independent operating-group calculations remain useful and valid. They show
how each group stands on its own and establish figures such as true cost,
breakeven rate and minimum required recovery without support from another
source.

Those calculations help define and explain the available modelling levers.
They are not, by themselves, the whole-business modelled outcome.

The whole-business result must still be produced by passing the selected changes
through the same isolated Business Outcome logic, including the applicable
allocation, revenue, recovery and reconciliation relationships.

Accordingly:

  - Independent calculations define each operating group's own economics.
  - Business Outcome shows the current whole-business state.
  - Business Modelling applies selected operating-group changes.
  - The isolated Outcome engine calculates the resulting whole-business state.

This preserves the value of the independent calculations without presenting
their aggregate as an alternative current-business truth.

===============================================================================
11. REQUIRED CURRENT-VERSUS-ADJUSTED PRESENTATION
===============================================================================

Business Modelling should explicitly show the relationship between the Outcome
baseline and the adjusted model.

The exact component design remains an implementation matter, but the conceptual
presentation should allow the user to understand, for each relevant operating
group:

  - what the current Business Outcome input or result is;
  - what the independent cost or breakeven requirement is;
  - what the user has changed;
  - what the adjusted input becomes;
  - what effect that adjustment has on the operating group; and
  - what effect all selected adjustments have on the whole business.

An underlying comparison table is a suitable concept. A possible structure is:

  Operating Group
  Current Rate
  Breakeven Rate
  Modelled Rate
  Current Hours / Utilisation
  Modelled Hours / Utilisation
  Current Revenue / Recovery
  Modelled Revenue / Recovery
  Current Contribution
  Modelled Contribution
  Variance

The table should not replace the existing lever controls. Its purpose is to make
the current-versus-adjusted relationship explicit and auditable.

The page should also clearly distinguish:

  Current / Baseline Outcome
  versus
  Adjusted / Modelled Outcome

The user should never have to infer which state a figure belongs to.

===============================================================================
12. ROLE OF LIVE DATA AND CURRENT ASSUMPTIONS
===============================================================================

Mirra currently knows part of the cause-and-effect chain from the existing
financial inputs, cost allocation, Rate Builder calculations and user-supplied
operating assumptions.

For example, the system can know:

  - the real annual cost assigned to a labour group;
  - the real annual cost assigned to an asset;
  - the current saved charge-out rate;
  - the calculated breakeven rate;
  - the assumed productive or running hours;
  - the resulting modelled revenue and recovery.

However, where actual time tracking or utilisation data is not yet available,
the system cannot conclusively determine whether a recovery issue is caused by:

  - an inadequate charge-out rate;
  - inadequate utilisation or productive hours;
  - an inaccurate operating assumption;
  - or a combination of these factors.

That limitation does not invalidate the present model. It means the current
model is an evidence-based baseline using explicit assumptions. Future live data
will test those assumptions and improve the confidence and diagnostic precision
of both Outcome and Modelling.

The system must continue to distinguish between:

  - imported or observed data;
  - calculated values;
  - user-entered assumptions; and
  - modelled adjustments.

===============================================================================
13. IMPACT ON THE BUSINESS MODELLING REBUILD PROPOSAL
===============================================================================

The earlier rebuild proposal should be refined to reflect the confirmed concept.

The proposal was correct to identify that Business Modelling must stop presenting
an independently aggregated result as though it were the current reconciled
business position.

However, the solution is not to create a new diagnostic layer duplicating
Business Outcome.

The refined direction is:

  1. Business Outcome remains the complete, trusted current-state and diagnostic
     module.

  2. Business Modelling imports or receives the current Business Outcome state
     as its locked Baseline.

  3. The user adjusts labour, asset and overhead recovery inputs at the operating-
     group level.

  4. The adjusted state is passed through the same Business Outcome module or
     calculation pipeline in isolation.

  5. Business Modelling displays the resulting adjusted whole-business position
     alongside the unchanged current Baseline.

  6. Independent source calculations remain available to establish each group's
     true cost, breakeven point and modelling options, but their aggregate must
     not substitute for the isolated whole-business Outcome calculation.

  7. Current-versus-adjusted values should be made explicit, potentially through
     an underlying comparison table and clear state labels.

  8. The baseline Business Outcome state must never be mutated by modelling.

This means the preferred conceptual structure is not:

  Section A: duplicate current-state diagnosis
  Section B: disconnected independent model

It is:

  Baseline: current Business Outcome state
  +
  Operating-group levers: explicit user adjustments
  +
  Isolated parallel Business Outcome: adjusted/modelled state
  +
  Comparison: current versus adjusted

===============================================================================
14. CONFIRMED DECISIONS
===============================================================================

The following decisions are confirmed from the discussion:

1. Business Outcome is the current solved state of the business.

2. Business Modelling is the future or adjusted state of the same business.

3. Business Modelling must begin from the Business Outcome Baseline.

4. Outcome and Modelling must use exactly the same underlying business engine,
   source contracts and reconciliation logic.

5. Modelling must use an isolated copy or state so it cannot alter the current
   Business Outcome Baseline.

6. Revenue, gross profit, net profit and cash are derived outputs, not the core
   modelling levers.

7. The primary business levers are Labour Recovery, Asset Recovery and Overhead
   Recovery, expressed through the operating groups established by Cost
   Allocation and Rate Builder.

8. Operating groups are the correct unit for modelling because they are how the
   business incurs cost, sets charge-out rates and generates revenue.

9. Business Outcome already supplies the current-state diagnosis. Business
   Modelling does not need a new diagnostic layer.

10. Independent calculations remain valuable for showing each operating group's
    own economics and breakeven requirements.

11. Independent source calculations must feed the modelling choices but must not
    replace the whole-business outcome engine.

12. The modelled state must be calculated by rerunning the same Business Outcome
    module or calculation chain against isolated adjusted inputs.

13. Baseline means the current Business Outcome state.

14. Breakeven is the minimum cost-covering reference point and may sit above or
    below the current Baseline.

15. Current and adjusted values should be displayed explicitly so the user can
    see what changed and the resulting business effect.

16. The single source of truth between Outcome and Modelling is non-negotiable.

17. Current assumptions remain legitimate inputs where live operational data is
    not yet available. Future live data will validate and refine those
    assumptions.

18. There are no arbitrary or fake headline metrics in the intended model. Every
    displayed outcome must be traceable to the specific business's financial
    data, operating costs, rates, assumptions and selected adjustments.

===============================================================================
15. CONCEPTUAL MODEL IN ONE STATEMENT
===============================================================================

Business Outcome is the reconciled current state of the business, derived from
its imported financial truth, cost allocation, Rate Builder, operating groups,
current rates and current operating assumptions. Business Modelling is a
controlled and isolated second layer that starts from that unchanged Outcome
Baseline, adjusts Labour Recovery, Asset Recovery and Overhead Recovery through
the same operating groups, and reruns the same Business Outcome engine to show
the resulting future or adjusted state. The purpose is to compare actual versus
adjusted business mechanics, not to model arbitrary accounting outputs. One
business engine and one source of truth must govern both states.

===============================================================================
16. IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY
===============================================================================

This record confirms the concept only.

It does not yet decide:

  - the exact React component structure;
  - the exact state-cloning mechanism;
  - the exact selector or hook changes;
  - the exact scenario storage format;
  - the final comparison-table design;
  - the treatment or retirement timing of legacy scenario tools;
  - the detailed implementation sequence;
  - the specific files to edit.

Those matters should be scoped only after the current repo wiring has been
verified against this conceptual decision.

Any implementation brief should demonstrate how it will preserve:

  - the unchanged Outcome Baseline;
  - isolated modelling state;
  - shared calculation logic;
  - explicit adjustments;
  - traceability from current to modelled values;
  - and reconciliation of the modelled result through the same Outcome engine.
