STATUS: DECISION NOTE - RETIRES S28, CLOSES ALL OPEN QUESTIONS FROM S26/S27
VERSION: v5.1
MODULE TYPE: Business Outcome / Commercial Truth Layer
DATE: 2026-08-28
AUTHORITY: Supersedes S28_RATE_BUILDER_PER_SOURCE_LINKING_v5_1.txt in full.

---

# 1. WHY S28 IS RETIRED

S28 was drafted on the assumption that a Rate Builder calculator needed to
be linkable to one specific physical asset (linked_asset_id) because two
assets sharing one calculator ("2inc Line," live data) appeared unable to
show individual profitability.

That assumption was tested directly against how the calculator is
actually used (user, this session, with screenshot evidence): "the
calculator is designed to represent how I would invoice this asset... it
represents how I would invoice." The "2inc Line calculator"'s three line
items (Setup / Hourly / m3 Rate) price the JOB TYPE - a 2-inch line
pumping job - not a specific physical pump. This is correct, intentional,
and matches real invoicing practice. It is not a data gap.

S28's proposed fix (relink calculators to individual assets) would have
broken something that already works correctly, to solve a problem that
has a much simpler real answer - see Section 2.

---

# 2. THE CORRECTED MECHANISM

## 2.1 What already exists, confirmed live this session

- The group's blended rate ($/hr) is already computed correctly:
  businessOutcomeAssetSplitCalculations.js:
    blendedRate = preview.total_charge / recoveryDriverQuantity
  Confirmed against live screenshot: $831.00 / 12 m3 job, decomposing to
  $207.75/hr total group rate at the group's 1920 recovery hours.

- Each individual physical asset already carries its OWN independent
  expected-hours figure, not a shared/group value:
  costAllocationSharedHelpers.js, get_total_asset_hours():
    row.asset_recovery_hours_used ?? row.utilisation_hours_annual ??
    row.utilisation_hours ?? row.available_asset_hours ?? 0
  Sourced per-row from asset_recovery_rows (Assets module), then carried
  through costAllocationAssetPoolBuilders.js's enriched_assignments as:
    assigned_asset_hours = available_asset_hours * (assignment_percent / 100)
  Confirmed live: "2inc Line" group's two real assets
  (bc1dc072-28f5-4812-885b-5c4033284549 and
  4b5eff75-3a86-4b52-b39b-111e433ee2fe) each have their own
  asset_group_assignments row, each independently 100% assigned - the
  underlying per-asset hours are real, not shared or derived.

- Cost is already fully per-asset: enriched_assignments also carries
  assigned_asset_cost per asset_id, already summed correctly into group
  totals by costAllocationGroupCostBuilder.js.

## 2.2 The fix

No calculator changes. No relinking. No migration. Apply the group's
already-correct blended rate to each asset's own already-correct hours,
inside the new Business Outcome hook only:

  asset_modelled_revenue (per asset_id) =
    group.blendedRate * that_asset's_own_assigned_asset_hours

  asset_true_cost (per asset_id) =
    that_asset's_own assigned_asset_cost (already exists)
    + that asset's fair share of the group's allocated overhead
      (assigned_overhead_amount, split by the same hours-share basis,
      since overhead has no existing per-asset field - see Section 4)

  asset_net_profit (per asset_id) = asset_modelled_revenue - asset_true_cost

This is READ-ONLY composition of numbers that already exist and are
already independently correct. Nothing upstream changes.

# 3. WHAT THIS NUMBER ACTUALLY IS - MANDATORY FRAMING

This is NOT a measurement of actual per-asset revenue. It is a MODEL:
real charge-out rate x real EXPECTED/TARGET hours (Assets module's
utilisation assumption), not actual invoiced hours or actual dollars
collected against that specific asset. This is the exact same category
of figure as useBusinessOutcomeLabourRecovery's already-existing
weighted_modelled_labour_revenue - modelled recovery capacity, not
history.

Confirmed directly by the user this session: "what we expect as
utilisation from the assets module and what we have from the P&L revenue
won't align - but that is exactly what we are trying to surface with the
tool." The gap between modelled and actual is the diagnostic, not an
error to eliminate. This reframes S23 Section 6's utilisation disclosure
requirement as applying to every individual per-source figure this new
view shows, not just the whole-business totals it originally covered.

Every per-asset (and per-labour-source) row in the new view must carry
the SAME persistent, non-buried disclosure S23 Section 6 already mandates
at whole-business level, e.g.:

  "Based on expected utilisation. May not match actual P&L revenue for
  this period - see reconciliation below."

# 4. OVERHEAD SPLIT WITHIN A MIXED-ASSET GROUP - NEW DECISION, NOT YET
   BUILT ANYWHERE

group.assigned_overhead_amount (from costAllocationGroupCostBuilder.js)
exists only at GROUP level - there is no per-asset overhead field
anywhere in the codebase, confirmed by every file traced this session.

Decision: split the group's assigned_overhead_amount across its
individual assets using the SAME basis as revenue - each asset's share of
total group assigned_asset_hours. Reason for consistency: overhead
recovery in this system is already hours-driven at the group level
(group_recovery_hour_source, group_cost_per_hour) - using the same driver
for the within-group split keeps the method internally consistent rather
than introducing a second, different allocation basis (e.g. cost-share)
for no stated reason.

This IS an allocation (unlike revenue and direct cost, which are real
per-asset numbers) and must be labelled as such wherever shown - e.g. "of
which $X is this asset's hours-based share of group overhead," not
presented as a directly-known figure.

---

# 5. RECONCILIATION ANCHOR - HOW THIS FITS S23/S26

Per source (labour and asset):
  modelled_revenue, true_cost, modelled_net_profit

Rolled up:
  sum(all sources' modelled_revenue) compared against total_revenue
    (Business Summary / P&L) - variance surfaced explicitly, per S23
    Section 3 / S26 Section 5. This variance is EXPECTED to be non-zero
    and is the actual point of the page, not a defect - see Section 3
    above. It must never be hidden, forced to zero, or smoothed into one
    source to appear to reconcile.

  sum(all sources' true_cost) - this side SHOULD reconcile close to
    total_cost_burden, since cost is real/actual on both sides (unlike
    revenue, which is modelled on the per-source side and actual on the
    P&L side). If total_cost does not reconcile, that IS a genuine leak
    per S23 Section 3 and must be treated as one - unlike the revenue
    variance, which is expected.

This is a materially important distinction for the build: the revenue
variance and the cost variance are not the same kind of number and must
not be displayed identically. One is an expected diagnostic finding: is
your business achieving what you priced for. The other is a data
integrity check.

# 6. WHAT REMAINS UNCHANGED FROM S26/S27

- Labour: reuse useBusinessOutcomeLabourRecovery's true_cost_per_hour and
  charge_out_rate directly (already correct, already per-source). Hours
  for revenue multiplication come from Cost Allocation's
  labour_group_assignments (assigned_hours per staff_type_id, summed
  across all groups that staff type appears in), NOT from Labour's
  productive_staff_type_rates.total_productive_hours - see S27 Section 2
  nuance, still valid, unaffected by this note.
- Unassigned labour/asset cost: reuse unassigned_labour_cost /
  unassigned_asset_cost directly from Cost Allocation's output_contract,
  confirmed live and load-bearing in businessOutcomeCalculations.js - own
  explicit reconciliation line, per S27 Decision 2.
- Residual overhead: totalBusinessOverheads minus the sum of every
  group's assigned_overhead_amount, computed fresh in the new hook (no
  existing passthrough field) - lands against materials, per S23 Section
  7 / S26 Decision 2(b), confirmed in S27.
- Materials/COG: as originally scoped in S26 Section 4, unchanged.

# 8. FINAL PRINCIPLE

The simplest fix was correct because it didn't try to change something
that already worked. The calculator prices the job the way the business
actually invoices it - that was never broken. What was missing was simply
connecting two numbers that already existed separately: a real rate and
each asset's own real expected hours. The harder-looking problem (assets
sharing a calculator) and the easier-looking fix (multiply existing
numbers together) turned out to be the same problem seen from two
different angles - the second one just required checking what the data
actually meant before deciding what to build.
