# 1. PURPOSE

This brief defines the core diagnostic feature of Business Outcome: the unpacking
of the single blended P&L Gross Profit and Net Profit figures into three true,
fully-loaded revenue streams.

It converts Business Outcome from a page that reports totals into a page that
proves where net profit actually comes from.

---

# 2. RECONCILIATION WITH S16 v5.0 (FLAG FOR REVIEW)

S16 (dated 2026-07-15) classified Cost Allocation, Recovery Summary, and Rate
Builder as "legacy Recovery Chain" routes and instructed Business Outcome to
source primarily from Cost Summary, Revenue/COG truth, Revenue Summary, and
Business Summary instead.

This session's design explicitly REACTIVATES Rate Builder and Cost Allocation
as required, named inputs to Business Outcome. The three-stream unpacking
described below is not possible without them.

This is flagged here rather than silently overridden. Before build begins,
confirm:

- Cost Allocation and Rate Builder are current, trusted, and in active use
  (not legacy/deprecated) in the present v5.0 build.
- If they are current, this brief updates S16's source chain accordingly.
- If they are not current, this feature cannot be built until they are
  reconciled or rebuilt.

---

# 3. CORE PRINCIPLE - THE RECONCILIATION ANCHOR

Total revenue shown on this page must equal P&L revenue.
Total net profit shown on this page must equal P&L net profit.

These are non-negotiable anchors, not modelling targets.

Everything else on this page is a re-slicing of those same totals using Rate
Builder, Cost Allocation, Labour, and Assets as the lens. No new revenue or
cost is created. No dollar is invented or discarded.

If the three stream figures do not sum back to the P&L totals exactly, this is
not a modelling choice. It is a leak, and it must be surfaced as an explicit
remainder. It must never be smoothed over, rounded away, or hidden.

---

# 4. WHY THE BLENDED GP/NET PROFIT NUMBER IS MISLEADING

Standard P&L structure:

Revenue - COGS = Gross Profit
Gross Profit - Labour Cost - Asset Cost - General Overheads = Net Profit

Labour cost and asset cost sit in operating costs, not COGS. But the revenue
charged for labour (Rate Builder labour rate) and the revenue charged for
asset use (Rate Builder / Cost Allocation asset rate) both flow into total
revenue.

The result: blended revenue is made up of three different things - material
sales revenue, labour charge-out revenue, and asset charge-out revenue - but
COGS only nets against the material piece. The blended GP figure therefore
compares blended revenue against only one-third of the true matching cost.
It cannot show whether any individual stream is actually profitable.

---

# 5. THE THREE STREAMS

1. Material / COG stream
   Revenue: material sales revenue
   Cost: true COGS
   Margin: standard material margin

2. Labour stream
   Revenue: labour charge-out revenue (Rate Builder labour rates)
   Cost: true labour cost (Labour module), plus its allocated share of
   overhead (Cost Allocation)
   Margin/Net Profit: fully-loaded labour net profit

3. Asset stream
   Revenue: asset charge-out revenue (Rate Builder / Cost Allocation asset
   rates)
   Cost: true asset cost (Assets module), plus its allocated share of
   overhead (Cost Allocation)
   Margin/Net Profit: fully-loaded asset net profit

---

# 6. THE UTILISATION ASSUMPTION (MANDATORY FLAG - HIGH PRIORITY, LOCKED)

Labour and asset margin/net profit figures are calculated on the assumption
that labour and assets are recovering at 100% utilisation. This is a
deliberate, conservative baseline choice:

- The business pays labour and asset cost in full regardless of actual
  utilisation (wages are paid weekly; asset finance/ownership cost accrues
  regardless of use).
- Assuming full recovery is the conservative direction: if real utilisation
  is lower, the model has already shown the best case, and any shortfall
  will surface as a gap once live data is available, rather than being
  hidden by an optimistic default.
- This is a baseline, not a proven figure. Precision on actual recovery
  comes later from live job/utilisation data (future build phase).

This must be displayed as a persistent, clearly visible disclosure directly
alongside every labour and asset margin/net profit figure on this page.
Suggested wording:

"Based on full utilisation assumption. Confirmed by live usage data in a
future release."

This is not optional and must not be buried in a tooltip or footnote only.

---

# 7. OVERHEAD DISTRIBUTION AND THE MATERIAL REMAINDER

Cost Allocation distributes a portion of General Overheads into the labour
and asset pools. What remains in the general overhead pool after that
distribution must land against the material stream, since material has no
dedicated Cost Allocation pool of its own.

This means material margin must be shown in two states:

- Material margin before the remaining overhead pull (= revenue - COGS)
- Material net profit after the remaining overhead pull (= material margin -
  residual overhead)

The post-overhead material figure, together with the fully-loaded labour and
asset net profit figures (already overhead-loaded via Cost Allocation), gives
three genuinely comparable, fully-loaded net profit figures - not gross
margins.

Sum of the three net profit figures must equal P&L net profit exactly (see
Section 3).

---

# 8. CROSS-SUBSIDY DETECTION

Because all three streams are now visible on a like-for-like, fully-loaded
basis, the page must surface when one stream is funding another - e.g.
material margin covering a shortfall in asset or labour recovery.

This must be shown as a plain-English statement with the dollar amount
attached, e.g.:

"Material margin is currently covering a $X shortfall in asset recovery
(based on the full-utilisation assumption)."

Do not rank streams against a "required recovery %" figure - that implies a
precision the system does not yet have without live utilisation data (see
Section 6). Cross-subsidy detection at this stage is a dollar-for-dollar
comparison of the three fully-loaded net profit figures, not a coverage-
percentage ranking.

---

# 9. DUAL VIEW: FULLY-LOADED NET PROFIT vs CONTRIBUTION MARGIN (TOGGLE)

In addition to the primary fully-loaded view (Sections 5-8), provide a
secondary, toggleable view that shows each stream's contribution margin
before any overhead allocation:

Contribution margin per stream = stream revenue - stream direct cost only
(no overhead distributed)

This view is allocation-methodology-independent. It strips out any
sensitivity to how Cost Allocation chooses to distribute overhead, and
instead answers: "how much does each stream throw off before overhead is
shared at all?"

This is directly analogous to a cash vs accrual toggle (e.g. Xero) - same
underlying data, two legitimate lenses answering different questions. Neither
view is wrong. The primary/default view is fully-loaded net profit; the
secondary view is contribution margin, one toggle away.

---

# 10. PAGE FRAMING

The page is framed around NET PROFIT, not revenue. Net profit is the number
that answers "is this business working." The revenue/cost/margin breakdown
per stream is the supporting proof underneath it, not the headline.

Recommended structure, top to bottom:

1. Total net profit (= P&L net profit, reconciliation-locked)
2. Three-stream net profit breakdown (material, labour, asset) - dollar and
   % of total net profit, shown together with revenue and cost for each
   stream at the SAME level (not hidden behind a drill-down click)
3. Utilisation assumption disclosure (Section 6) - persistent, adjacent to
   labour and asset figures
4. Cross-subsidy statement (Section 8), where applicable
5. Fully-loaded vs contribution margin toggle (Section 9)
6. Drill-down detail per stream (existing micro-level build: cost, charge,
   margin detail already built for labour/assets - this page collates them,
   it does not replace them)

Revenue and margin must appear together at the top level for each stream.
Margin must not be a drill-down-only detail - a stream that looks large by
revenue but thin by margin must be visible at a glance, not discovered only
after a click.

# 11. REQUIRED INPUT CONTRACTS

Business Outcome must consume:

## Rate Builder
- labour charge-out rate(s)
- asset charge-out rate(s)
- rate_builder_status / model_trust_state

## Cost Allocation
- working groups / group cost stacks
- overhead distributed into labour pool
- overhead distributed into asset pool
- residual (undistributed) general overhead pool
- allocation_reconciliation_checks
- cost_allocation_status / model_trust_state

## Labour module
- true labour cost

## Assets module
- true asset cost
- utilisation data where available (future live-data phase)

## P&L / Revenue-COG truth
- total_revenue
- total_COG
- gross_profit
- net_profit (anchor values - Section 3)

## Cost Summary
- total_business_overheads (pre-distribution, for residual calculation)

These must be consumed through structured outputs or selectors, per existing
system principle. Business Outcome must not mutate any of them.

---

# 12. REQUIRED OUTPUT CONTRACT

Minimum required fields:

- output_contract_version
- source_period
- reconciliation_status (pass/fail against P&L anchor)
- total_revenue (= P&L revenue)
- total_net_profit (= P&L net profit)
- remainder_amount (must be zero; if not, flagged as blocking warning)

Per stream (material, labour, asset):
- stream_revenue
- stream_revenue_percent_of_total
- stream_direct_cost
- stream_allocated_overhead (labour/asset only; material = residual pool)
- stream_contribution_margin (pre-overhead)
- stream_net_profit (fully-loaded)
- stream_net_profit_percent_of_total
- utilisation_assumption_flag (labour/asset only)
- data_quality_state

- cross_subsidy_statement (text + dollar amount, where applicable)
- view_mode (fully_loaded | contribution_margin)
- warning_list

Where a field is not currently calculable, it must be returned as
unavailable or deferred rather than invented.

---

# 13. BOUNDARIES

Business Outcome must not:

- invent or adjust revenue/cost/net profit totals to force reconciliation
- hide a remainder if the three streams do not sum to the P&L anchor
- rank streams using a coverage-percentage that isn't yet supported by live
  data
- treat the utilisation assumption as fact rather than a flagged assumption
- mutate Rate Builder, Cost Allocation, Labour, or Assets source data
- collapse the fully-loaded and contribution-margin views into one number
- bury the utilisation assumption disclosure in secondary UI (tooltip only)

---

# 14. RELATIONSHIP TO EXISTING BUILT COMPONENTS

The micro-level build (click into labour, click into an asset, and see its
cost/charge/margin) already exists and is retained as-is. This brief adds the
missing collated top-level view that sits above those micro views and
presents all three streams together, reconciled to the P&L, as one net
profit story.

---

# 15. FUTURE PHASE (NOT IN THIS BUILD)

- Live utilisation/job data replacing the 100% assumption with actual
  recovery percentages
- Coverage-percentage-based stream ranking once actual data supports it
- Cash-basis P&L import (explicitly out of scope - see session notes;
  accrual is the correct and only basis for this feature)

---

# 16. DEFINITION OF DONE

Business Outcome net profit unpacking is ready when:

- Total revenue and total net profit reconcile exactly to the P&L, with any
  remainder surfaced, never hidden
- Three streams (material, labour, asset) each show revenue, cost, and
  net profit together at top level
- Utilisation assumption disclosure is persistent and adjacent to labour and
  asset figures
- Residual overhead correctly lands against material after Cost Allocation's
  distribution to labour and asset pools
- Cross-subsidy statement generates correctly when one stream is funding
  another
- Fully-loaded vs contribution-margin toggle is available and both views
  reconcile independently
- Section 2 reconciliation with S16 is resolved (Rate Builder / Cost
  Allocation status confirmed as current, not legacy)

---

# 17. FINAL PRINCIPLE

One blended GP and Net Profit number tells you nothing about which part of
the business is actually working. This page reverse-engineers the P&L's net
profit into three honest, fully-loaded streams - material, labour, and asset
- so the business owner can see exactly which part of the business is
carrying the others, and which part is being carried.

END OF BRIEF
