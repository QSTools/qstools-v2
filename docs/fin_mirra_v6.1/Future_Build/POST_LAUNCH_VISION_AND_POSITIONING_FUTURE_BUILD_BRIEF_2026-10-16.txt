2. EARLY FEEDBACK AND WHAT IT CHANGES
===============================================================================
  - FACT (owner): a couple of people have been shown the tool. Their comment: it is too complicated to understand and
    use.
  - OWNER CONSTRAINT: there is a floor to how far the tool can be simplified before it stops working properly,
    because the chain is genuinely connected: labour feeds cost allocation, which feeds rate builder, business
    outcome and business modelling. Flattening the chain would make the numbers wrong.
  - CONSEQUENCE (agreed): the complexity stays in the engine; the EXPERIENCE is simplified. That is the job of the UI
    tidy-up (R5) and above all the AI layer (R7): setup coaching to get the right data into the right fields, and an
    explainer that turns Business Outcome and Business Modelling into plain language. This is why the AI layer is inside
    the launch bar rather than a nice extra.
  - The owner also said the system has grown past what he can hold in his head, and that he agrees to things that he
    learns on the fly in discussion. The answer recorded: the briefs, the trace tool and the regression suite are the
    external memory; the agent will later do the same job for users. Read the launch brief and the kickoff rather than
    trusting recall.

3. ARCHITECTURE: DETERMINISTIC CORE, SEPARATE AGENT LAYER
===============================================================================
  - The core tool (labour, cost allocation, rate builder, business outcome, business modelling) is deterministic
    calculation. Same inputs, same outputs, no judgement. It is verified, not trusted. It must never become "AI does
    the maths".
  - The explainer is an agent. It reads stored node outputs and explains them; it never computes (stress brief D-8). A
    what-if goes through Business Modelling's own lever calculation and is labelled as not actual.
  - Two front doors, ONE dependency chain. The explainer cannot exist without the core tool's numbers, but it can be
    reached from somewhere other than the tool's own screens. Idea (owner): the tool listed in the Xero App Store, and
    the agent separately surfaced wherever agents are found. Because the agent reads stored data through a fixed set of
    functions (stress brief 7.2), it is not welded to one UI, which keeps that option open at no extra cost.
  - STATUS OF THE CLAIMS: that agent marketplaces will be a meaningful channel, and that this shape is common (payments
    and commerce platforms exposing an engine behind separate front doors, and companies publishing agent-accessible
    interfaces to their data), was offered from general knowledge in the session and not researched. Treat as a strategic
    bet, not a plan. Re-check before building any marketplace-facing work.
  - GATE: the Xero developer terms (2 March 2026) on AI/ML use of Xero API data apply to any agent reading
    Xero-derived data, wherever it is distributed. Read them first (GO_TO_MARKET Q2).

4. PROACTIVE "PART-TIME CFO": WEEKLY DIGEST AND DRIFT DETECTION (post-launch idea)
===============================================================================
  Idea (owner): the most valuable extension is the agent noticing drift and reporting weekly, the way a CFO would:
  here is how you are tracking this month, this is not quite right, that is not quite right. Over time it builds a
  pattern of behaviour ("labour cost creeps up each time you take on this type of job, and this is the third time").
  - It is the same capability as the explainer on a schedule: push instead of wait.
  - The pattern part needs HISTORY, which is why every calculation node is stored and time-stamped (launch Addendum 1,
    A4; stress brief D-7). A snapshot alone shows a blip, not a pattern.
  - PROPOSAL (not decided), consistent with D-8: drift is detected by deterministic code comparing stored nodes over
    time against thresholds, and the agent only narrates what the code flagged. The agent must not decide that a
    number has drifted by estimating.
  - Hard part: deciding what counts as drift, per business, without noise. Needs real data from real users first.
  - Dependencies: the database, stored nodes with dates, the explainer, the Xero terms check, scheduling.
  - Not on the launch path.

5. THE LEDGER LOOP (owner's insight)
===============================================================================
  Most tools built on an accounting platform are one-directional: they read the ledger and show something nicer.
  The owner's design closes a loop:
    1. Use the P&L (the ledger) to set the baseline of how the business is actually performing.
    2. Model a profitable outcome on that baseline (Business Modelling).
    3. Run purchase orders and timesheets through the tool. They generate granular, correctly categorised records.
    4. Feed those back so they form the ledger. The business then tracks against its baseline.
  Why it matters (reasoning, not research): the tool's need for accurate operational data gives the owner a reason to
  enter it accurately, and the result lands cleanly in the ledger instead of being reconciled by hand later.
  Caveats:
    - It depends on R8 (purchase orders, Xero write-back, timekeeping), which is AFTER launch. The first release
      delivers steps 1 and 2 only, plus explanation.
    - Write-back brings the full Xero certification scope (account and payment mapping, taxes, webhooks); see the
      GO_TO_MARKET brief Section 4.
    - The no-reproduction rule applies: do not import or write data just because it is available.
    - The claim that nobody else does this is the owner's impression from looking around. It has not been researched
      here.

6. EDGE ASSESSMENT (reasoning from the session, NOT competitor research)
===============================================================================
  Three things together, which the owner may be able to defend longer than any one alone:
    1. Calculation depth and connectedness: a verified chain from labour to business modelling that reconciles. Hard
       to build and hard to trust. It is a moat only as long as it stays ahead of copies.
    2. A trust posture: the ability to show a number is right and keep proving it (trace tool and regression suite).
       Slow to build, unglamorous, does not demo well, and is the kind of thing a faster, better-funded competitor is
       likely to cut. Reasoning only.
    3. An architecture that lets an agent explain trusted numbers, distributed separately from the tool (Section 3).
       An agent with no trustworthy engine behind it is a chatbot guessing at numbers.
  Against Xero: Xero's core is a ledger, which is regulated and must be right, but it records what happened. The owner's
  tool answers a different question (what should a job cost and charge, and is it recovering true cost), which a
  ledger does not need to answer. That is why it extends Xero instead of competing with it. This is reasoning from
  the session, not a statement about Xero's engine, which was not examined.
  Why the gap might exist (HYPOTHESES): software teams without deep operations knowledge; domain experts who do not
  build software; low willingness to pay per customer in trades and small services, so funded companies chase larger
  simpler contracts; verification work is unglamorous and is cut first under growth pressure. None tested.
