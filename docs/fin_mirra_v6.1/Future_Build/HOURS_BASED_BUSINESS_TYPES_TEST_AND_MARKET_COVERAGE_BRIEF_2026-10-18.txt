-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY THIS LIST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mirra's hours mode fits any business that sells TIME (people's hours, machine hours, or both). The market the tool can
capture at launch is the set of those businesses it handles correctly. Two shapes are proven so far (civil/earthmoving,
crane/plant hire, plus an over-staffed variant). Each new type is picked because it pushes a part of the chain the earlier
ones barely touched, so passing it widens the market the tool can honestly serve.
Businesses that sell UNITS (cafes, retail, manufacturers, per-item producers) wait for units mode (R4).

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE TYPES (grouped by shape)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Columns: examples | shape | what it stresses | likely gaps to check | priority (1 = next).

T1  PLANT AND OPERATORS (asset-heavy, operator tied to machine)                         STATUS: PROVEN (Scenario A, B)
    Civil and earthmoving, drainage, crane hire, drilling, demolition, forestry and logging crews.
    Shape: high-value machines, operator per machine, moderate COGS.
    Stresses: asset-hours units, labour cap, idle cost, cascades, shared assets.
    Gaps: dry hire (F-7, in the fix batch).

T2  TRANSPORT AND LOGISTICS                                                             PRIORITY 1
    Courier, owner-driver freight, trucking, removals, towing, shuttle and taxi operators.
    Shape: one driver per vehicle, fuel and road user charges large, small fixed overheads, growth = more vehicles and
    drivers.
    Stresses: vehicle running costs into the asset overhead pool, owner who drives (owner as PRODUCTIVE staff), small
    business scaling up (3 to 8 vans as two recipes), thin margins.
    Gaps: contract drivers paid per job (cost of sales, not wages); leased vehicles (overhead line, not an owned asset);
    per-drop or per-km billing (units mode; convert to an hourly equivalent until R4).
    Real-world check available: the owner's friend's courier business (with his permission, anonymised, :3004 only).

T3  RESIDENTIAL BUILDER / MAIN CONTRACTOR (COGS-heavy)                                  PRIORITY 2
    House builders, renovators, fit-out and joinery contractors, commercial main contractors.
    Shape: materials and subcontractors 40-55 percent of revenue, own carpenters on hours, project margin on materials.
    Stresses: materials markup, the materials row in View A and View B, direct-cost categories beyond materials
    (subcontract labour, hired plant, waste), a business whose profit sits mostly in cost of sales.
    Gaps: subcontractor margin vs own-labour margin; work-in-progress timing (P&L year vs job timing) - note only.

T4  TRADE SERVICE (van, tools, materials at markup)                                     PRIORITY 3
    Plumbers, electricians, HVAC, painters, roofers, glaziers, landscapers, locksmiths.
    Shape: tradesperson + van + apprentice, materials resold at markup (15-30 percent of revenue), call-out work.
    Stresses: junior/apprentice staff type, van as a support asset assigned to a crew (F-4 rule), materials at a mid share,
    many small units (one per tradesperson).
    Gaps: travel time as non-billable hours (productivity percent covers it?); call-out fee as revenue not tied to hours.

T5  PROFESSIONAL SERVICES (labour only)                                                 PRIORITY 4
    Accountants and bookkeepers (also Mirra's sales channel), engineers, architects, quantity surveyors, surveyors,
    lawyers, IT managed services, marketing agencies.
    Shape: no productive assets, high salaries, billable utilisation 50-75 percent, heavy partner / non-billable time.
    Stresses: labour-only groups throughout, low productivity percent, non-productive cost as the largest pool, charge-out
    rates far above wage.
    Gaps: partners who bill but also manage (split productive / non-productive per person); fixed-fee work (hourly
    equivalent).

T6  HIGH-HEADCOUNT MOBILE SERVICES (low rate, many part-timers)                         PRIORITY 5
    Commercial cleaning, security guarding, home care and support work, labour hire, lawn and garden rounds, pest control.
    Shape: many staff on low rates, part-time and casual mix, few assets (vans), the "high headcount" route to a $100M
    business.
    Stresses: varied hours_per_week and days_per_week across hundreds of staff, staff list performance (2c), ACC classes.
    Gaps: casual staff paid holiday pay as 8 percent on top (not leave weeks) - check the Labour module supports it;
    shift and weekend penalty rates.

T7  WORKSHOP SERVICES (bays, technicians, parts)                                        PRIORITY 6
    Mechanics and auto repair, panel beaters, engineering and fabrication job shops, marine and small-engine repair.
    Shape: workshop bays / hoists / machines as assets used by technicians, parts resold at markup, labour rate per hour.
    Stresses: assets tied to a bay rather than a person, parts as COGS at markup, several technicians sharing one machine
    (the "together" pattern).
    Gaps: shop consumables vs parts; warranty / non-billable rework hours.

T8  APPOINTMENT-BASED PERSONAL AND HEALTH SERVICES                                      PRIORITY 7
    Physiotherapy, chiropractors, dental hygiene, beauty and hair salons, massage, tattoo studios.
    Shape: practitioner + room or chair (the asset), appointment hours, some retail product.
    Stresses: room / chair as a productive asset shared across practitioners, practitioners as contractors (rent a chair).
    Gaps: retail product sales (units, R4); contractor practitioners paying a fee to the business.

T9  SEASONAL AND RURAL CONTRACTORS                                                      PRIORITY 8
    Agricultural contractors (harvesting, baling, spraying), shearing gangs, fruit-picking and orchard contractors.
    Shape: big machines used hard for part of the year, seasonal and casual staff, long idle season.
    Stresses: asset weeks override far below 48, seasonal shutdown, idle cost over a long off-season.
    Gaps: seasonal staff on part-year contracts (paid weeks < 52) - check Labour supports it.

T10 EQUIPMENT HIRE, NO OPERATOR (dry hire)                                              BLOCKED ON F-7
    Kennards / Hirepool style tool and equipment hire, scaffolding, portable toilets, generators and lighting towers.
    Shape: many assets, few staff (yard, mechanics), hourly machine rates (weekly / monthly hire later in R4).
    Stresses: the F-7 exemption at volume, support staff only, asset count at scale (thousands of small assets).
    Gaps: F-7 itself; day / week rates (R4).

T11 SOLE TRADER (edge case, every type)                                                 PRIORITY: run alongside T2
    One person, one vehicle or none, almost no overheads.
    Stresses: single-row groups and cascades, divide-by-near-zero, a business where the owner IS the only productive staff.

4. TEST ORDER AND METHOD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Order: T2 courier (with T11 sole trader), T3 builder, T4 trades, T5 professional, T6 high-headcount, T7 workshop,
  T8 appointments, T9 seasonal, T10 after F-7.
  Each type: a recipe from a rough shape (about ten decisions), aligned P&L first, the independent expected script,
  a live run, a wording read of Business Outcome as an owner would read it, then one scale step if the type has a scale
  story. Any gap found becomes a numbered finding with an owner decision, same as F-1 to F-12.
  Before writing each recipe, check the "Gaps" line against the tool (read the module) so a missing feature is found as
  a finding, not as a failed comparison.
  Real data: only with the business owner's permission, names stripped, on the isolated :3004 instance. A synthetic
  recipe of the same shape comes first; the real P&L is then a second check.
