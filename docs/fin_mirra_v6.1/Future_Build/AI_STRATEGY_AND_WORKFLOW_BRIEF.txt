PART A - STRATEGY: WHERE MIRRA STANDS AGAINST THE AI TALKS
===============================================================================

A.1 WHAT THE TALKS SAID (priority order for Mirra)
 C1  Moat is unique IP / data, not software sold by the seat. Anyone can describe standard software to an AI and get
     a version in minutes. "A bit better, faster, cheaper" is not IP.
 C2  Do not publish; sell. Publishing gives models the advantage.
 C3  AI does not know the consequences of its actions: a human buffer is needed. Use AI to learn, not to avoid learning.
 C4  Rebuild around AI; do not bolt it on.
 C5  Agents do the tedious work nobody gets to (example: a cheese company's agent checks carrier invoices against
     box size and files credit requests; reported saving about $50,000 a month).
 C6  Agents drift between model versions, so they need maintenance.
 C7  Iterate continuously. C8 Personalised teaching/explanation. C9 Curiosity. C10 Talk to AI at length.
 Eon podcast: models and compute are ephemeral, data is the durable asset. Second-order risk, parked: AI agents as a
   non-human security/compliance threat class once Mirra holds a real database of client data (revisit at R6).
 Eisman podcast: the moat test is switching cost. Mirra is a moat only if leaving costs the client something real
   (their own calculated history), not because the logic is clever.
 Not relevant to Mirra (do not re-read for them): foundation-model spending, world models, lab leadership, SaaS
   selloff beyond C1, spam, jobs outlook.

A.2 WHERE MIRRA ALREADY MATCHES [PK]
 M1  Moat is the calculation chain and its judgment calls (covered hours capped by labour; overhead split by cost
     share; non-productive cost policy; Best Case vs How the Business Runs), not the screens.
 M2  Human stays the buffer: Apply-Patch with mandatory confirmations, live tests on real data, stop-and-confirm on core
     calculation files, git status before commit.
 M3  Dev process is AI-native. Whether the PRODUCT is built that way for users is open (R7).
 M4  One reconciled output contract, many consumers (Business Outcome contract feeds AI Export, Dashboard, Business
     Modelling).
 M5  Shows its working: calculation trace, CALCULATION_MAP, VARIABLE_TRACKER.
 M6  Governing principle: the tool exists to reveal gaps; a gap that is calculated but not shown breaks the promise.
     An agent would extend "shown" from "visible if you look" to "pushed to you".
 M7  Partial: a health gauge explainer exists; it does not adapt to the reader.

A.3 GAPS
 G1  End-user agent / alerting. Nothing runs without a person loading a page. Needs (a) a trigger (the chain runs
     client-side; see A.5), (b) a comparison - persisted history of calculated outputs, which does not exist (first-time
     storage design), (c) delivery. THE HARD PART IS THE RULE, NOT THE PLUMBING: what gap, over what window, deserves to
     interrupt an owner. NOT DEFINED. Candidate inputs already calculated: capacity coverage gap dollar value, a unit
     tipping from viable to loss-making, non-productive cost share spike, unexplained_revenue.
 G2  Explanation adapted to the reader (owner vs accountant). Idea only.
 G3  Adviser / consultant use of Mirra with clients. Business model question. Parked.

A.4 RISKS AND CHECKS
 R-1 Keep the moat private: briefs, CALCULATION_MAP, VARIABLE_TRACKER and the logic are the valuable part. (Exposure
     found in A.5; AI Export resolved in A.5.)
 R-2 Generic CRUD gives no protection: judge new features on whether they add proprietary judgment or only screens.
 R-3 Conventions rule 10 is the same question as C1: do not import data just because it is available.
 R-4 Alert fatigue (G1). R-5 Model drift if any feature ever depends on an external model (none today).

A.5 VERIFIED BY CODE READ, 2026-10-14 [VERIFIED]
 - The whole calculation chain (40+ files in lib/calculations/) ships into the browser bundle: every calculation hook
   is "use client". app/api/ had exactly three routes (acc/search, demo-login, pnl-import), none touching the chain.
   The moat exposure is live, not hypothetical.
 - AI Export (hooks/useAiBusinessState.js, buildAiBusinessStateExport) only feeds app/ai-business-state/page.jsx, a
   read-only JSON preview. No AI SDK in package.json. Nothing leaves the app. Revisit only if AI Export is ever wired
   to a third-party model.

A.6 DECISIONS MADE (2026-10-14)
 - Server migration: move calculation / selector files server-side, whole files only, never split mid-logic. Entry
   point is a thin dispatcher (app/api/calculate/route.js + lookup table), inside the ~600-line convention.
 - Units before launch: build units mode fully before the database exists, so shared calculation changes do not tangle
   live financial history. Accepted cost about 6 months. (Now R4 in the build order.)

A.7 THE THIRD MOAT - ANONYMISED CROSS-CLIENT BENCHMARKING
 - Layers: (1) a business's own modelled-vs-actual history (the switching-cost moat; part of the database step);
   (2) cross-business snapshot vs anonymised peers; (3) market trend over time. Layers 2-3 are the only network effect
   identified; sequenced AFTER layer 1 and after the database.
 - Already exists, no new calculation logic: Business Outcome per_source, capacity_coverage_gap, Labour Recovery rates,
   Recovery Summary stream shares, Cost Summary required_recovery_rate, revenue, industry/trade.
 - Genuinely new: a stored consent flag (+ timestamp, scoped to anonymised ratios only, never raw data, never model
   training); size-band bucketing; one aggregation module outside the existing chain, reading finished output-contract
   data and writing its own storage. HARD DEPENDENCY: the database must exist with real cross-client rows.
 - Sample floor: roughly 20-30 businesses per category x size band for directional signal (rigorous 200-385). Go deep in
   one trade first (construction is the leading candidate, not locked in).
 - Valuation research: data-moat companies roughly 5-10x revenue vs 2-5x for comparable SaaS without one. Answer to
   "how do I stay ahead of a copier": copying is inevitable; client history and the benchmark pool cannot be copied,
   only built over time.
 - LEGAL GATE (hard, not optional): price-signaling risk (RealPage, Agri Stats precedents; Gibson v. Cendyn favourable).
   Design direction, NOT lawyer-cleared: show a BAND not a number; historical/monthly, never live; sample floor; state
   where the business sits against the band and stop, never recommend a rate or action; never market it as "the market
   rate". A lawyer must review band width, sample floor, cadence and exact UI copy BEFORE it is built.
