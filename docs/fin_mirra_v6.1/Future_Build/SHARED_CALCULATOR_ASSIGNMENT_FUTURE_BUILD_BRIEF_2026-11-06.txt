===============================================================================
1. PROBLEM (owner's words in plain terms)
===============================================================================
A fleet of machines doing the same job is charged at ONE rate, but each machine cost a different amount because it was bought at a different time: five five-tonne diggers (a Komatsu and a Cat have different names, different purchase dates, different finance) all charged at 120 an hour; twelve concrete mixer bowls bought a year apart, one price per cube; a fuel transporter with trucks of mixed age on one rate per litre. Today every asset needs its own calculator (RATE_BUILDER_BRIEF L9: each asset gets its own calculator, the group price is the SUM of its assets' prices, a result and never an input). Twelve identical calculators is tedious and invites mistakes.

===============================================================================
2. DECISION (owner, 2026-11-06)
===============================================================================
D1  One drop-down under each asset in Rate Builder with two choices: CREATE NEW CALCULATOR (how it works today) or USE EXISTING CALCULATOR (a list of calculators already built). Picking an existing one links the asset to it: the asset shares that calculator's PRICE and keeps its OWN COST (own finance, own pool share, own hours).
D2  No separate "assign" tab. A tab was proposed first (calculator first, then tick the assets); rejected because Rate Builder already lists every Cost Allocation item in a drop-down and prices them one at a time, so the flow is asset first, calculator second.
D3  A "quantity on the asset record" was REJECTED (bowls or diggers bought later have different price and finance; see UNITS_MODE Addendum 1 item 9).
D4  A single calculator pricing a whole group with a multiplier was REJECTED as the main route: it shows only the fleet margin, and the group hours rule (asset hours = MAX of the group, COST_ALLOCATION_BRIEF R4) does not give per-machine hours.
D5  Feature is visible in every business mode: a Komatsu and a Cat are two different assets doing the same job, so "second asset with the same name" is the wrong trigger. The drop-down is always there; a business with one asset simply picks create new.

4. DESIGN REQUIREMENTS (PROPOSED, ask before any storage field)
===============================================================================
R1  The link is a new field on the asset-linked calculator record (name TBD). Owner's explicit yes on the stored shape before any build.
R2  A linked asset keeps its own cost. The shared calculator's rate is PER ASSET HOUR: recovery hours are each asset's own, never summed across the linked assets (owner: "obviously").
R3  If an asset is linked to a shared calculator its own calculator is hidden, so nobody prices it twice by accident.
R4  [OPEN] May one asset sit under two calculators (a digger charged at one rate with a bucket and another with a breaker)? Leaning allow; owner decides.
R5  Real revenue stays tied to the P&L. Only the MODELLED revenue per source is built differently (Rate Builder and Cost Allocation are how the tool gets to that figure). Totals must still reconcile to the P&L; that check does not change. Fewer assets should show as unpriced.
R6  The L11 hard block (labour incomplete locks add and edit) still applies to the shared calculator.
R7  Model mode: the Rate Builder rate lever and the scenario Rate Builder note read calculators; confirm they resolve a shared calculator (compare BUSINESS_MODELLING_BRIEF Addendum 35 item 9, which checked the charge-out rate and materials markup reads, not the calculators).
