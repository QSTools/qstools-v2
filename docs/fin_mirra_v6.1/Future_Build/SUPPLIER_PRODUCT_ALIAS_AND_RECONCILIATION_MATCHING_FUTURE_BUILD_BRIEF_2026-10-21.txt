1. THE PROBLEM
===============================================================================
Every supplier names the same thing differently. The example given was
concrete: many concrete companies, many names and codes on their quotes, POs
and invoices, but underneath it is one product type (for example a 25 MPa mix
at a given aggregate size and slump). Reconciliation can only compare a PO line
with an invoice line if both resolve to the same canonical product, unit and
price basis. "Concrete" is the assumed meaning in this brief; the pattern is
the same for any trade supplies.

===============================================================================
2. RULES CARRIED IN FROM THE MIRRA DATABASE WORK (owner, 2026-10-16)
===============================================================================
  - One multi-tenant database; a tenant key on every row holding business data;
    row-level isolation enforced in the database. One business must never see
    another's data.
  - Every calculated number is stored, not recomputed on demand.
  - AI never produces a number the user treats as true. Variance amounts are
    plain deterministic code. AI may only SUGGEST a product match, and a person
    confirms it (Section 5).
  - Stable ID on every record; relate by ID, never by name or array position.

===============================================================================
3. PROPOSED TABLES
===============================================================================
  tenant_id sits on every table below unless marked REFERENCE.

  supplier
    supplier_id, tenant_id, name, tax/company number, external_id (for example
    the Xero contact id), status.

  canonical_product
    canonical_product_id, tenant_id (or REFERENCE, see decision A), name,
    category, standard_unit (for example m3), attributes (strength, size,
    slump or other spec as structured fields, not free text), is_active.

  supplier_product_alias            (the link that does the work)
    alias_id, tenant_id, supplier_id, raw_text (exactly as the supplier wrote
    it), raw_code (their product code, if any), canonical_product_id,
    unit_conversion (from their billing unit to standard_unit, with the factor
    stored as a number, for example per load to m3), match_source (rule,
    ai_suggestion, person), confirmed_by, confirmed_at, confidence (for
    suggestions only), status (suggested, confirmed, rejected, retired).
    Unique on tenant, supplier and raw_code or normalised raw_text.

  po_line and invoice_line
    line_id, document_id, supplier_id, raw_description (NEVER overwritten),
    raw_code, quantity_raw, unit_raw, unit_price_raw, canonical_product_id
    (nullable until matched), alias_id (which mapping was used), quantity_std
    and unit_price_std (converted using the alias, stored).

  supplier_price_history
    tenant_id, supplier_id, canonical_product_id, effective_date, price per
    standard unit, source document. Gives "are we being overcharged" and
    price drift without extra work.

  reconciliation_result             (stored output, per rule D-7)
    result_id, tenant_id, po_line_id, receipt_or_docket_id (if used),
    invoice_line_id, status (matched, quantity_variance, price_variance,
    both, unmatched, over_tolerance), quantity_variance, price_variance,
    variance_amount, tolerance_used, computed_at, computed_from (what inputs and
    which alias version), resolved_by, resolved_at.

===============================================================================
4. WHY KEEP THE RAW TEXT AS WELL AS THE MAPPING
===============================================================================
Disputes, audits and wrong mappings all need what the supplier actually wrote.
If a mapping is later found wrong, the raw text lets every affected line be
found and re-resolved. Retire an alias rather than deleting it, so history
still explains old results.

===============================================================================
5. MATCHING FLOW
===============================================================================
  1. Exact hit on supplier + raw_code, or supplier + normalised raw_text, in a
     CONFIRMED alias: link the line automatically.
  2. No hit: a fuzzy or AI step proposes a canonical product with a confidence.
     It writes a "suggested" alias only. It never links a line by itself.
  3. A person confirms or rejects. Confirming sets confirmed_by, confirmed_at
     and links every waiting line from that supplier with the same text.
  4. Anything still unmatched stays in an "unmapped" queue, visible, not passed.
  5. Reconciliation runs only on linked lines. Unlinked lines are reported as
     unmatched, never guessed.
  Result: each supplier's quirks are resolved once, then remembered.

===============================================================================
6. DECISIONS THAT CHANGE THE DESIGN
===============================================================================
  A. Whose canonical list? Per business, or one shared list across all
     businesses. A shared list speeds onboarding but is cross-tenant data. It
     would need to be read-only reference data with no business-specific rows.
     Aliases stay per business either way, because they show who a business
     buys from.
  B. Units and price basis. Per m3, per load, per tonne, delivery or pump
     charges, minimum-load fees. Which are separate lines and which fold into
     the product price?
  C. Tolerance. Quantity and price tolerance (percent or amount), per supplier
     or global. Needs an owner decision; do not default silently.
  D. Match depth. Two-way (PO to invoice) or three-way (PO to delivery docket
     to invoice). Concrete usually has a docket, so three-way is likely worth it.
  E. Versioning. When a mapping or conversion changes, do old results restate?
     Proposed: no. Old results keep the alias version they used.
  F. Xero. If invoices round-trip through Xero, account and payment mapping and
     invoice webhooks apply (see the 2026-10-16 go-to-market brief, Section 4).
     Check the developer terms before sending supplier invoice text to any AI
     step (its Q2).

===============================================================================
7. OUT OF SCOPE / FIRST SLICE
===============================================================================
Out of scope: any code, choosing a database host, the OCR or invoice reading
step, the user interface, price negotiation features.
First slice when picked up: take 20 to 30 real invoice lines from three
suppliers of one product, write them out by hand against a draft of the tables
above, and see where the model breaks (units, delivery charges, odd codes).
Only then fix the schema.
