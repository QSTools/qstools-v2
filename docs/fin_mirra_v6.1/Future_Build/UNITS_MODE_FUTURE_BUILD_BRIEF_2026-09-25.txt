1. WHY THIS EXISTS / THE CORE WORRY THAT STARTED IT
===============================================================================
The whole tool (Cost Allocation, Business Outcome, Business Modelling's new
floor/start/ceiling model) is built hours-native: covered hours, revenue hours,
recovery hours, assignment percentages of time. The owner's worry: building
Business Modelling's floor/start/ceiling model now, for hours, then having to
reopen and redesign it later for a units-based business (e.g. product sales,
SaaS subscriptions) - effectively tripling the workload.

CONCLUSION REACHED (important, changes the risk profile): this worry is largely
UNFOUNDED, once traced through properly. floor/start/ceiling, true cost, pro-rata
shortfall distribution, and Cost Allocation's group-and-assignment mechanism are
ALL basis-agnostic - they don't care whether the denominator is hours or units,
they just divide a true cost by "however much of this thing there is." The
actual gap is narrow: (a) some fields are currently one-name-does-one-thing
(e.g. get_group_covered_hours assumes hours), and (b) two genuinely new pieces
are needed for the SaaS/zero-labour case specifically (see Section 4).
This means units mode does NOT require doubling the Business Modelling rebuild.
It requires: Cost Allocation gaining a second valid recovery basis (unit count,
alongside hours), and Rate Builder gaining a per-unit pricing path (flat and
tiered) alongside its existing hourly calculator. Business Outcome and Business
Modelling's floor/start/ceiling logic downstream should work unchanged once fed
a true-cost-per-unit instead of a true-cost-per-hour - THIS IS AN ASSUMPTION,
not yet proven against real code; first task of the actual session is to verify
it rather than assume it (see Section 8).

3. THE CORE MECHANISM DECISION (how units mode should work, conceptually)
===============================================================================
DECIDED: Cost Allocation does NOT need to change its fundamental mechanism.
It already does exactly what it needs to: take real costs (labour, asset,
overhead), distribute them into a working group via assignment shares, and
produce one true annual cost for that group. Whether that group's eventual
rate is "per hour" or "per unit" is invisible to Cost Allocation - it just
produces a total dollar figure. The SAME assignment-percentage mechanism
already used for labour/asset hours works unchanged for unit shares (e.g. "the
Screen Printing group's cost splits 30% Pants / 70% T-Shirts" is structurally
identical to "PC55 gets 50% of the Operator's hours").

THE ENTIRE UNITS-MODE CHANGE LIVES IN RATE BUILDER: instead of dividing a
group's true annual cost by hours to get an hourly rate, Rate Builder needs a
new path that divides by a manually-entered unit count (invoices processed,
t-shirts printed, subscriptions sold) to get a true cost PER UNIT, then prices
from there - either a flat per-unit markup (reusing the existing materials-
markup pattern: cost x (1 + markup%)) or a tiered plan price (new, see Section
4). Business Outcome / Business Modelling's floor/start/ceiling logic should
then work off "true cost per unit" and "price per unit" exactly as it works
off "true cost per hour" and "charge-out rate per hour" today - ASSUMED, not
verified against real downstream code yet (see Section 8, first task).

EXCEPTION / GENUINE COST ALLOCATION CHANGE STILL NEEDED: a group with NO
productive labour and NO productive assets (e.g. a pure SaaS OCR service - see
Section 5) has nothing for Cost Allocation's hours-based recovery mechanism to
divide by; recovery hours would be zero and the tool would produce nonsense
or divide-by-zero. Cost Allocation DOES need a genuine new recovery basis
option for a group: "unit count" as an alternative to "labour hours" / "asset
hours", so a group with a real cost but zero productive hours can still get a
non-zero, meaningful recovery basis. This is the one place Cost Allocation
itself needs new logic, not just new inputs.

4. WORKED EXAMPLE 1 - T-SHIRTS AND PANTS (simple per-unit pricing, product
   business, existing mechanism mostly sufficient)
===============================================================================
Setup: one operating group ("Clothing"), shared labour (press operator) and
overhead. Two working-unit lines underneath it, T-Shirts and Pants, each with
an assignment share of the group's capacity (e.g. "Pants get 30% of the
press's output") - structurally identical to today's labour/asset assignment
percentage mechanism, just relabelled as a quantity share instead of an hours
share. Cost Allocation distributes the group's true cost across the two lines
by that share. Rate Builder then prices each line off its own true cost per
unit, using a pattern similar to the existing materials markup tab (cost per
unit x markup).

KNOWN, ACCEPTED LIMITATION (do not try to solve this - explicitly out of
scope, discussed and closed): this only works at the LINE level (T-Shirts vs
Pants), not per-SKU. If "Pants" contains many different styles at genuinely
different costs (cargo pants vs plain pants), Cost Allocation has no mechanism
to see inside a line and split further - it would give one blended average
true cost for the whole "Pants" line. For a business with a small, stable
number of distinct product lines (a handful), this is fine. For a high-SKU
retailer (owner's own example: "if you were Bunnings" - thousands of
constantly-changing items, a $5 packet of screws next to a $3,000 laser in
the same group), this mechanism genuinely does not and should not try to work.
DECIDED: Cost Allocation's full chain (Cost Allocation -> Business Outcome ->
Business Modelling) is explicitly scoped for "a business with a few distinct,
reasonably stable product or service lines" - NOT high-SKU retail. That is a
different category of problem (retail inventory / per-SKU margin management)
and is out of scope for this tool, not a gap to be closed later.

IMPORTANT SEPARATE FINDING FROM THIS DISCUSSION: Rate Builder's
cost-per-unit-times-markup mechanism (the existing materials markup pattern)
is DECOUPLED from Cost Allocation - it works standalone for any single item's
margin check regardless of volume. This means a high-SKU retailer, explicitly
out of scope for the full Cost-Allocation-through-Business-Outcome chain,
could still get value from Rate Builder's markup tool used standalone, item by
item, without ever touching Cost Allocation. Worth remembering as a "what CAN
this tool still offer a high-SKU business" answer if ever asked, without
implying the full chain will ever support them.

5. WORKED EXAMPLE 2 - SAAS / TIERED USAGE PRICING (own tool as the test case;
   genuinely new mechanism needed, not just relabelling)
===============================================================================
Setup discussed: the tool (Fin Mirror) pricing itself. Two working groups:
  - "Basic Subscription": one person/flat-fee basis, near-zero marginal cost
    per additional user. Straightforward - effectively today's mechanism
    applies with unit count = 1 subscription. No new mechanism needed here.
  - "OCR" (invoice-reading via Azure Document Intelligence, used to
    auto-reconcile invoices against POs): NO labour attached at all - pure
    external per-page vendor cost. This is the case that breaks the existing
    hours-based recovery mechanism (see Section 3's "exception" above) and
    exposes a genuinely missing cost-input concept (below).

5A. AZURE OCR PRICING - FACT-CHECKED LIVE 2026-09-25 (web search, keep for
    reference, re-verify pricing if this is ever actually built - rates
    change): flat pay-as-you-go per page, NOT tiered by volume on Azure's own
    side. ~$1.50 per 1,000 pages for basic Read/OCR model; ~$10 per 1,000
    pages for the prebuilt Invoice model (the one actually relevant here).
    Azure's own "commitment tiers" are a discount for committing to volume
    upfront (Azure's cost to you, not a thing to model), NOT usage-based
    tiers charged to your own customers. CONCLUSION: no tiering complexity on
    the input/cost side - it's a flat rate per page. Any tiering is entirely
    the SELLER'S (Fin Mirror's) own pricing strategy layered on top of a flat
    underlying cost, not something forced by the vendor's pricing shape.

5B. THE MISSING COST-INPUT CONCEPT (genuine gap, needs a real design decision,
    not yet made): there is currently NO field anywhere in the tool for "a
    manually-entered external per-unit vendor cost" (e.g. $0.01/page from
    Azure). Every existing cost input comes from one of three places: Labour
    (needs a person + hours), Assets (needs a purchase price + finance
    terms), or the P&L import (a real historical total, already blended into
    whatever category it landed in). None of these fit "I know my exact
    per-unit rate from a vendor's published pricing, but it's not separately
    broken out in my P&L, and even if it were, that's a TOTAL not a per-unit
    rate."
    IMPORTANT DISTINCTION SURFACED AND RESOLVED IN THIS SESSION (do not
    reopen without cause - this is the actual answer): these are TWO SEPARATE
    problems that were initially conflated:
      (i) Getting the right TOTAL DOLLAR cost into Cost Allocation for the
          OCR group. RESOLVED: if you have been trading and the Azure cost is
          already inside your real P&L (even blended into a generic
          "Subscriptions" line), it is ALREADY flowing through General
          Overheads and Cost Allocation correctly in TOTAL DOLLAR terms - no
          new input mechanism needed for this part at all. Splitting your P&L
          so Azure has its own line (rather than being buried in a blended
          Subscriptions line) is just better bookkeeping/categorisation,
          entirely outside this tool, not a tool change.
      (ii) Getting a PER-UNIT rate (cost per page / per invoice) to build a
          price against. RESOLVED: this is NOT a cost problem at all, it's a
          UNIT ECONOMICS INPUT - you already know it (from Azure's own
          pricing page, or by dividing your real total OCR cost by how many
          invoices you actually processed that year). The fix is NOT a new
          cost category or module. It's Rate Builder needing a place to enter
          "how many units did this group's true cost actually produce" (a
          real, user-entered unit count), so Rate Builder can do
          true_cost_per_unit = group's true annual cost (already correctly
          computed by Cost Allocation, unchanged) / unit count (new input,
          entered in Rate Builder). NO new module, no new cost category
          alongside Labour/Assets - just one new input field in Rate Builder's
          units-mode pricing path, dividing an already-correct total.
    AN EARLIER IDEA DURING THIS DISCUSSION - "add a whole new cost module
    alongside Labour/Assets for external per-unit vendor costs" - was
    considered and then SUPERSEDED by the resolution above. Do not rebuild
    that earlier idea; the P&L-already-has-it-as-a-total-plus-Rate-Builder-
    divides-by-a-unit-count answer is the one to build from.

5C. TIERED PRICING - NEW MECHANISM, GENUINELY DOES NOT EXIST TODAY (Rate
    Builder's calculator today = rate x quantity, one number per unit; a
    tiered plan is NOT pricing per unit, it's pricing per PLAN - a flat
    monthly fee bundling an allowance of units). Worked example given by
    owner (arbitrary numbers): 250 invoices/month for $100; 500 for $150;
    1,000 for $200. REQUIRED NEW BUILDING BLOCK: a tiered-plan pricing tool
    in Rate Builder, separate from (but alongside) the simple flat
    per-unit-times-markup tool, that does:
      - user-defined tier BOUNDARIES and PRICES as inputs (NOT hardcoded to
        three tiers, or any fixed number - arbitrary count and values, fully
        configurable by the owner/user per plan)
      - margin at FULL/WORST-CASE usage per tier (plan fee minus true cost at
        that tier's included volume ceiling) - this is the safety/floor check
      - margin at TYPICAL/EXPECTED usage per tier (owner raised using a
        Pareto-style distribution assumption - e.g. "most customers on the
        500-1000 tier actually only use 500-700" - to get a realistic, not
        worst-case, margin picture). This needs a way to capture or estimate
        that expected-usage distribution per tier; NOT yet designed HOW
        (manual estimate entry? historical usage data if/when the tool has
        real customers on it? open question, not answered this session).
    This tiered tool does not need Cost Allocation to change at all beyond
    the unit-count recovery basis in Section 3's exception - it is entirely a
    Rate Builder-side pricing mechanism sitting on top of an already-correct
    true-cost-per-unit figure.

6. RELATED BUT SEPARATE FINDING - OPERATING GROUP CAPACITY UTILISATION
   (BIG, LOGGED AS ITS OWN FUTURE ITEM - NOT PART OF UNITS MODE SPECIFICALLY)
===============================================================================
Raised by the owner as a related but distinct idea while discussing tier
customer-distribution percentages: every operating group in the tool TODAY
implicitly assumes it is running at its full, accounted-for capacity. If a
group (e.g. a digger assigned 100% to a working unit) is actually only
earning revenue some percentage of that assigned capacity (e.g. genuinely
utilised/earning 60% of the time), that gap is currently INVISIBLE anywhere in
the tool unless someone manually and explicitly captures it. This is a real,
structural blind spot across the ENTIRE tool, not specific to units mode - it
applies equally to every hours-based group today.
OWNER'S OWN ASSESSMENT: likely why this is hard, and possibly why similar
tools don't attempt it - most users probably couldn't honestly answer "what
percentage is each group actually running at" even if asked.
CONNECTION TO UNITS MODE: the tiered-plan customer-distribution percentage
(Section 5C's Pareto idea - what % of customers land in which tier) is
STRUCTURALLY THE SAME KIND OF INPUT as an operating-group utilisation
percentage would be - both are "what fraction of theoretical capacity is
actually realised" questions.
DECISION: log this as its OWN, SEPARATE, standalone future design item -
larger in scope than units mode alone (applies to every group, hours or
units). Do NOT fold it into the units-mode build. Revisit only as a
deliberate, separate future session once units mode (and everything ahead of
it) is done.

7. WHAT UNITS MODE NEEDS TO BUILD - CONSOLIDATED LIST (this is the actual
   scope, once that future session starts)
===============================================================================
A. Cost Allocation: add "unit count" as a second valid recovery basis for a
   group, alongside labour hours / asset hours, specifically for groups with
   zero productive labour and zero productive assets (Section 3 exception,
   Section 5B). Existing assignment-percentage mechanism for splitting a
   group's cost across multiple lines (Section 4's T-Shirts/Pants) should NOT
   need new code - confirm this live rather than assume (see Section 8).
B. Wire the two confirmed-dead-end hops (Section 2): Recovery Summary's
   unit_type/average_sale_rate/revenue_share_percent -> Cost Allocation, and
   Cost Allocation (or Recovery Summary directly) -> Rate Builder. Trace and
   decide the cleanest hop path live in that session; do not assume Recovery
   Summary is necessarily the right intermediary just because it's where data
   currently stops.
C. Rate Builder - simple per-unit pricing path: reuse the existing materials-
   markup pattern (cost per unit x markup%) for a units-mode working unit,
   analogous to how the hourly calculator prices an hours-mode working unit.
D. Rate Builder - NEW tiered-plan pricing tool (Section 5C): user-defined
   tier boundaries/prices (arbitrary count), margin at full/worst-case usage
   per tier, margin at typical/expected usage per tier (distribution
   mechanism TBD - open question, not designed).
E. Rate Builder - unit-count input for a units-mode working unit, letting the
   tool compute true_cost_per_unit = group's already-correct true annual cost
   (from Cost Allocation, unchanged mechanism) / user-entered unit count
   (Section 5B(ii)). NOT a new cost category or module - confirmed resolution,
   do not rebuild the earlier "new module" idea.
F. Business Outcome / Business Modelling downstream: VERIFY (not assumed) that
   floor/start/ceiling, true cost, and the additive lever model work unchanged
   once fed a true-cost-per-unit and price-per-unit instead of true-cost-per-
   hour and charge-out-rate-per-hour. This is the first concrete task of the
   actual build session (Section 8) - trace the real code, don't assume the
   analogy holds everywhere (e.g. get_group_covered_hours and every other
   hours-named field in useBusinessOutcomePerSourceRevenue.js explicitly
   assumes hours today - see CALCULATION_MAP_v6_0.txt s9.1-9.2 - these would
   need to become basis-aware, reading either a covered-hours or a
   covered-units figure depending on the group's mode).
NOT IN SCOPE (explicitly, per Section 4 and Section 6):
  - Per-SKU / high-volume retail granularity (Section 4's Bunnings example) -
    permanently out of scope for the full chain, not a future phase of units
    mode.
  - Operating group capacity utilisation / realised-vs-assigned-capacity gap
    (Section 6) - real, but a separate, larger, standalone future item.
