===============================================================================
1. THE IDEA
===============================================================================
Two linked uses of one forward-looking layer:
  A. INTERNAL (first): Mirra measures its own business. Used by the owner and shown to the mentor and later investors.
  B. CUSTOMER-FACING (parked): a growth / diagnostic view for a tradie: if I win more hours, raise my rate, or add a
     machine, where am I in twelve months, and what is the business realistically worth by the right yardstick for its
     type. The tool today explains the COST side (true cost per hour, utilisation, who makes or loses money); this adds
     the FORWARD side.

===============================================================================

===============================================================================
  - Wants the tool to run its own growth model "as part of the tool", measured against what was discussed.
  - Sees it as useful to any user, not only Mirra.

===============================================================================
3. FIRST VERSION (INTERNAL) - WHAT TO MEASURE
===============================================================================
Inputs the owner types each month (nothing is pulled from customers at this stage):
  paying customers; new customers won; customers cancelled; price per customer per month; revenue; cost of delivering
  the service (the COGS lines in GO_TO_MARKET Addendum 2 item 1, including AI usage, hosting, database, payment fees,
  Xero tier); owner hours by category (sales, setup, support, build).
Outputs (rule-of-thumb benchmarks quoted in session are [GENERAL], not researched; show them as labelled
assumptions the owner can change):
  - monthly revenue and growth rate (fast = roughly 50 to 100 percent a year or more)
  - monthly cancellation rate (healthy roughly under 1 to 2 percent a month)
  - cost to win a customer (spend plus owner hours valued at a fair rate, divided by new paying customers) and payback
    months against the price
  - gross margin and profit; months of runway
  - customers and months to reach 100, 1,000, then the revenue levels in the mentor's question (1M, 20M)
  - owner hours logged against each stage
Projection: simple month-by-month from a few levers (win rate, price, cancellations, cost). It answers "what would it
take to go from 1M to 20M" by changing inputs. Revenue, profit and cash only; no valuation in v1.

===============================================================================
4. DESIGN RULES (AGREED IN SESSION)
===============================================================================
  1. Keep it SEPARATE from the verified calculation engine. The engine reconciles to the dollar; this layer contains
     guesses. No shared state with the calculation chain; no figure from here feeds Cost Allocation, Business Outcome or
     Business Modelling.
  2. Assumptions always visible, labelled as assumptions, never presented as a forecast promise.
  3. Start as a spreadsheet or a simple internal page, NOT inside the customer-facing app, so it does not distract from
     R3 and the real-data run. A spreadsheet satisfies the mentor's question today.
  4. Dogfood: once the Mirra company has its own P&L in the tool, the company's COGS and overheads come from the tool,
     not by hand (GO_TO_MARKET Addendum 2 items 1 and 2).
  5. Follow the AI advice rule (show options, never "you should") if the AI layer ever reads this view.
  6. New calculated figures need a calculation trace node and calc map entry if it is ever built into the app
     (conventions definition of done).

5. CUSTOMER-FACING VERSION (PARKED UNTIL THE CORE IS PROVEN)
===============================================================================
  - Forward view per business: levers over twelve months (hours won, rate, add a machine), extending Business Modelling.
    Likely a Business Modelling extension, not a new module; decide at design time.
  - Valuation by business type [GENERAL, UNCHECKED rules of thumb]: service and construction businesses are commonly
    valued on a multiple of PROFIT (about 2 to 4 times) or well under 1 times revenue; software on a multiple of
    REVENUE (3 to 5 times at small scale, 5 to 10 times with fast growth and low cancellations). The yardstick and the
    ranges must be checked against sources before any customer sees a number. Risk: a valuation figure shown to a
    tradie is read as advice or a promise. Needs lawyer review and a clear "indicative only" frame.
  - What lifts value (utilisation, margin, recurring work) can be drawn from the existing outputs.
  - Needs jurisdiction and business-type awareness (GO_TO_MARKET Addendum 3 item 14; HOURS_BASED brief).
