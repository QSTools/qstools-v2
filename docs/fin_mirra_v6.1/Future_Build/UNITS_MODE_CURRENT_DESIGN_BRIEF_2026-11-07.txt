1. WHAT UNITS MODE IS, AND THE CHAIN AT A GLANCE
===============================================================================
Hours mode prices and tests a business by the hour. Units mode does the same job for a business that sells a countable thing (cubes of concrete, tonnes, litres, cups, kilometres, a load) so that the answer is "how many units must we sell, at what price, to pay for everything". The engine underneath is mostly basis-agnostic: cubes play the role hours play, as the denominator. Cubes are an outcome (demand) where hours are a capacity (a ceiling), and the working unit changes from the asset hour to a LOAD.

The chain, for the concrete reseller (single product, single unit first):
  1. Setup page: pick units mode (business_type product_based). The setup page is the mode switch only.
  2. P&L: imported as normal, unchanged.
  3. Assets: each bowl gets a CAPACITY (one value, one unit: the maximum load). Twelve bowls stay twelve asset records.
  4. Labour, General Overheads, Opening Hours, Cost Summary, Business Summary: unchanged. Cost Summary and Business Summary stay on the opening-hours basis.
  5. Revenue / COGS: the existing unit row holds the SELL side (unit, average sale rate; cubes sold = revenue / rate). A new PURCHASED ITEMS LIST holds the BUY side (concrete per cube, hired truck per load).
  6. Cost Allocation: a working group assigns the bowl plus the cost of sales lines (concrete, truck) as a fourth kind of assignment; overhead is laid over the top as today. Cost Allocation holds no figures.
  7. Rate Builder: one calculator line, the price per cube; many bowls share it ("use existing calculator").
  8. Business Outcome: cubes sold, cost per cube, price per cube, break-even cubes (display layer first, trace node on every figure).
  9. Model mode (later): what-if on volume and on buy/sell price; scenarios for adding or replacing a truck; the "when to grow" number.

2. DECISIONS BY MODULE
===============================================================================
SETUP
 - DECISION: mode switch only (labour_based / product_based). "Hours vs units" is naming drift from the code (VARIABLE_TRACKER P2.1). Units are NOT chosen here; they are picked per row in Revenue / COGS because a business can sell in several units. No new stored field.
 - FINDING: Business Outcome never reads business_type, so in product mode it still speaks hours. Product mode has been wiring-checked only and never run with numbers (RC-3).

REVENUE / COGS
 - DECISION: the purchased items list is the BUY side only: name, unit, price paid per unit (concrete about 200 a cube; hired truck about 240 a load). There is no new sale price list: the sell side is the existing unit row (unit_type, average_sale_rate, revenue_share_percent). A list line's unit is picked from the same unit list as the unit rows.
 - OPEN, MUST DECIDE BEFORE ANY BUILD (rule conflict): REVENUE_COGS R2, R7 and Section 10 say cost-of-sales numbers come from the P&L and are not stored in Revenue / COGS. So the list must be a RATE CARD: price x volume gives an EXPECTED cost of sales, shown beside the P&L figure with a reconciliation line, never replacing it. Owner decides the framing and where the volume is entered. A new stored list is a new stored field: explicit yes first.
 - DECISION: volume is DERIVED, never typed: cubes sold = P&L revenue / average sale rate (R5). A volume what-if is a Model mode lever, not a typed number on this page.
 - DECISION: the share of revenue per stream is a typed estimate on the unit rows (revenue_share_percent; warning when the shares do not add to 100), flagged unmeasured on screen. The P&L gives total revenue only, so nothing in the books can check the split. The LIVE LAYER (invoice lines) later replaces it with measured figures.
 - DECISION: HOURS IS A UNIT. "hour" is already a unit_type (m2, m3, lm, tonne, hour, each, custom). A pure hours business stays on hours mode unchanged. A business that mixes picks units mode and uses hours as one unit. mixed_unit_based exists but has never been run with numbers. The engine branch for an hours row inside units mode is UNBUILT.
 - FINDING: kilometre, litre, load and cup are not on the unit list (custom is the workaround). OPEN whether to add them.
 - PARKED: a supplier label on each list line. Xero payables per supplier by month give dollars with no quantities, so spend cannot become volume without purchase orders and invoice lines.

ASSETS
 - DECISION: capacity per trip is ONE value and ONE unit per asset, the MAXIMUM load, a fixed property of the machine (about 10 tonnes for a cartage truck, 20,000 litres for a tanker, 4.8 to 6 cubes for a bowl). Shown only in product mode. How full each load runs is a separate UTILISATION question, parked.
 - DECISION (default, may change): the capacity unit is picked from the same unit list as the purchased items, so the two cannot drift.
 - DECISION: no quantity field on an asset (rejected: bowls bought at different times differ in price and finance). Twelve bowls = twelve asset records.
 - New asset field: explicit yes first; bug class (b): add it to every explicit field list or it is silently dropped.
 - OPEN: an asset with no trip (a coffee machine) may need a rate per hour instead of a load; one field with two meanings or two fields.

COST ALLOCATION
 - DECISION: Cost Allocation only ASSIGNS and holds no figures. Each number is entered once, in the module that owns it.
 - DECISION: the cost of sales link LIVES in Cost Allocation, as a FOURTH kind of assignment beside labour, assets and overhead. A working group assigns the bowl (the only productive asset), the concrete line and the truck line; Cost Allocation multiplies capacity x buy price. Non-productive staff (owner, spouse, dispatcher) reach the group through overhead as today. New stored shape: explicit yes first.
 - DECISION: a LOAD is the working unit. Concrete cost per load = capacity x buy price; hired truck per load = its load rate.
 - DECISION: hired-in truck and driver are COST OF SALES, not Labour (LABOUR_BRIEF Section 10; a zero on-cost contractor row would show Labour not ready). If drivers have a guaranteed weekly minimum the cost is fixed instead: ask the business. The dry-hire tick is outbound only and cannot represent hired-in.
 - DIRECTION: each bowl gets its OWN working group inside the same operating group (twelve groups), not one group for all twelve (one group distorts per-hour rates: group asset hours are the MAX of the assets, the labour cap is MIN). The repeated setup is solved by a DUPLICATE WORKING GROUP button: own brief DUPLICATE_WORKING_GROUP_FUTURE_BUILD_BRIEF_2026-11-07.txt, not units specific.
 - MUST FIX IN THE BUILD: C3b - in product mode overhead_absorbed_cost = total cost burden, so labour and assets count twice per group.
 - OPEN: does overhead spread by recovered hours (today) or by volume in units mode? With one large fixed cost over many groups this is an owner decision.
 - DESIGN: with hours as a unit, one operating group can hold working groups of different kinds side by side: (a) hours only (goods supplied by the customer; today's engine), (b) load rate (truck assigned plus a load rate from the list; a per-tonne rate falls out of load rate / capacity), (c) hours plus material (material buy and sell from the list, truck by the hour).

RATE BUILDER
 - DECISION: one price per cube covers concrete AND delivery ("he is just selling cubes"). The calculator for the working group carries ONE line, the price per cube (about 250). It does no cost build-up; the cost comes from the other modules. No delivered price split (for example 235 and 15) for the reseller.
 - DIRECTION: the price fills in automatically from the Revenue / COGS average sale rate; an override per job is allowed later, not in the first version. Confirm before build.
 - DECISION: shared pricing across assets is the SHARED_CALCULATOR_ASSIGNMENT brief (one drop-down per asset: create new or use existing calculator; the asset keeps its own cost and shares the price). Not units specific.
 - PARKED: customer price tiers (cost per load is the floor; one average price per cube first). Part loads: a half load still carries the full fixed cost, so it needs its own entered rate (a pricing tier), parked.
 - PARKED, NEW INPUT: HOURS PER LOAD (cycle time), needed to turn an hourly truck rate into a cost per tonne. A build in its own right, after the concrete core. The first version handles per-load and per-unit cost lines only.

BUSINESS OUTCOME
 - DECISION: show cubes. Required revenue (Cost Summary total cost burden plus cost of sales) and actual revenue (P&L), each divided by cubes sold, give break-even versus actual price per cube. Cheapest first version is a DISPLAY LAYER on the untouched hours engine. Every figure shown (cubes sold, cost per cube, price per cube, break-even cubes) gets a trace node and a calc map entry.
 - The mode flows one way down from setup; each stage reads it. There is no single switch node.

MODEL MODE (BUSINESS MODELLING)
 - DECISION: two kinds of change. A LEVER changes a number that exists (sale price, buy price, overhead percentage, charge-out rate). A SCENARIO adds or replaces something that does not (a truck, a driver on PAYE, a person; a new plant is a different design, later).
 - FINDING: Model mode today is hours only and does not handle cost of sales. A cubes lever and a COGS lever do not exist. A COGS lever widens the Tier 1 list (today General Overheads and Rate Builder only): explicit owner yes first (BUSINESS_MODELLING Addenda 38 and 41).
 - DECISION: buying a truck is a scenario with ADD or REPLACE. Add = more capacity, nothing switched off. Replace = the hired line comes off and an owned asset plus a driver on PAYE goes on, so the scenario must retire the hired line or the truck is paid twice. Hired is variable cost; owned is fixed cost.
 - DECISION, HEADLINE FEATURE: the "WHEN TO GROW" number (technical name: affordability threshold). For a proposed purchase (a person, a digger, a truck, a bowl and driver, even a non-productive ute): how much does the WHOLE BUSINESS need to be doing, consistently, before it is safe? Method: existing fixed cost plus new fixed cost, divided by margin per unit, compared with what the business does now; the gap is the safety margin. Works in BOTH modes, parked behind units mode, core first. OPEN: does "safe" mean bare break-even or break-even plus a profit the owner wants to keep (recommend showing both); "consistently" needs a period basis, which only the live layer can measure. Arithmetic wording only, no "should". Prominent in the UI. Details: BUSINESS_MODELLING Addendum 41.

3. WORKED EXAMPLE - CONCRETE RESELLER (ILLUSTRATION, NOT A TOOL RESULT)
===============================================================================
Assumptions (the outside owner has not yet supplied real figures): sell about 250 a cube, buy about 200 a cube, hired truck about 240 a load (about 120 an hour for a two hour cycle), bowl capacity 4.6 to 6 cubes, twelve bowls at about 100,000 each, three non-productive people.
 - Per load at 6 cubes: concrete 1,200 + truck 240 = 1,440 direct cost; 240 a cube against 250, so 10 a cube is left to pay for the bowl, overhead and profit.
 - At 4.8 cubes a load the load is exactly break-even at those prices (0.00 margin); at 4.6 it is -10 a load; at 5.6 it is +40. Break-even BUY price with a 200 load: 206.52 at 4.6 cubes, 214.29 at 5.6 cubes. (Hand numbers from the test case brief.)
 - With an assumed 1,000,000 a year of fixed cost (a round number for ease), break-even is about 100,000 cubes a year; at 20,000 cubes the fixed cost is about 50 a cube. This is why the output cannot be a per-load price: the back end of the business is a per-cube figure that depends on volume.
 - Buying at 200 and selling at 250 leaves 50 a cube; everything else (truck, bowl finance, overhead, the three people) comes out of that 50.

4. GENERALISATION (NOT BUILT; AFTER THE CONCRETE BUILD)
===============================================================================
 - TRANSPORT: any carrier fits the same shape (capacity per trip, a load, cost per unit). The buy list is OPTIONAL (a pure carrier never buys the goods). Pay bases seen: per tonne plus hours, per load, hours only. Milk carrier paid per kilometre (owner-driver owns the truck, the milk company owns the trailer, wage paid after fuel and maintenance): units mode, one asset (the truck, no capacity), one labour person, one unit row in kilometres, no buy list.
 - RETAIL: a single-product reseller is the structural core of retail (buy, sell, margin per unit, fixed cost / margin = break-even units). PER-SKU RETAIL STAYS OUT OF SCOPE (Section 8). A POS import to reduce a menu to an average margin is the later route; check whether an import is necessary first (standing rule 10).
 - COFFEE CART: cart and machine are two assets in one working group; units are cups; cost of sales is beans and milk. A YIELD is needed where the buy unit differs from the sale unit (a 1 kg bag at 15 g a shot is about 66 shots): an optional field on a purchased item, sale units per buy unit. Opening hours become a sanity CEILING, not an input: break-even cups a year / open hours = cups per hour needed, compared with what the machine can make.
 - SAAS AND TIERED USAGE PRICING (from the 2026-09-25 scoping, still stands): a group with no productive labour and no productive assets (a pure per-page vendor cost) has nothing for the hours-based recovery to divide by, so Cost Allocation needs "unit count" as a second recovery basis. Rate Builder needs a per-unit path (cost per unit x markup) and a tiered-plan pricing tool (user-defined tier boundaries and prices, margin at worst-case and at typical usage per tier; how typical usage is entered is undesigned). Azure OCR pricing noted in the original brief must be re-verified if ever built.
 - ORIGINAL SCOPE ITEMS A TO F (still stand): A unit count as recovery basis; B wire unit data from Recovery Summary to Cost Allocation and Rate Builder; C Rate Builder per-unit path; D tiered-plan tool; E unit-count input; F verify Business Outcome and Business Modelling work unchanged on a per-unit basis (get_group_covered_hours and other hours-named fields would need to become basis-aware). Added by the reseller design: G purchased items list, H capacity per trip on the asset, I cost of sales as a fourth assignment, J per-cube display in Business Outcome, K COGS lever in Model mode.

5. SETUP BY INTERVIEW (FUTURE; SEE AI_ASSISTED_SETUP BRIEF, ADDENDUM 2026-11-07)
===============================================================================
 - DIRECTION: on sign-up the AI interviews the owner, one plain question at a time, to establish the SHAPE of the business. A shape is not a box; it is independent answers: what do you sell and in what unit; do you buy the goods or only carry or make them; how are you paid (per unit, load, hour, kilometre); what carries or makes it (own asset, hired-in, customer's); what limits how much you can sell (trips, machine capacity, opening hours, staff hours; may change by day or job); fixed calendar or on demand.
 - RULES: the interview SETS UP, the TOOL ANSWERS. The AI never works out or states a break-even or any result, never invents numbers, notes "no idea" as unknown, shows each answer back for confirmation, and writes into the fields that already own the data (no new storage for the interview itself).
 - DESIGN RULES (owner: it must stay very simple): one question per number, asked once, in the owner's words; required versus optional (capacity, yield, cycle time and split are optional and never block the answer); thin slices with a test case each; a new field joins the core only when the concrete reseller or the coffee cart forces it.
