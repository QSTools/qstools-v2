===============================================================================
1. PURPOSE
===============================================================================
Business Modelling lets the owner change genuine decisions (overheads, charge-out rates, unit rates, markup) and
see the effect on net profit against TODAY's real position. The method (owner, confirmed): walk down the chain in
order - overheads first (they move every floor), then labour rates, then assets and materials - and see the result.
Every source is shown against three markers:
  FLOOR   breakeven (full true cost + non-productive share per covered hour; 0% for materials)
  START   current achieved rate from Business Outcome (View A)
  CEILING the entered rate x the hours it can cover (not a cap - a ceiling can sit below the floor)

===============================================================================
2. OWNS / DOES NOT OWN
===============================================================================
OWNS: lever state (qs_tools_business_modelling_levers_v1); the today / modelled / change display; source rows built
from per_source; the additive model.
DOES NOT OWN: any business calculation - it reads Business Outcome's per_source (and General Overheads' grouped rows,
read-only). Never writes to a source module. Never overrides Model Readiness. Never infers missing source truth.

===============================================================================
3. RULES (do not break)
===============================================================================
R1  TODAY is never mutated. With no levers set, modelled == today exactly.
R2  Revenue, net profit and cash are consequences, never levers. Genuine levers: charge-out rates, unit rates,
    markup, undistributed overhead lines, staffing / hours, financing. NEVER levers: employer KiwiSaver (hard-locked),
    ESCT, ACC (constrained to a small legislated band at most), insurance premiums, market fuel prices.
R3  Touch one lever -> only that source changes; every other source stays at today's achieved figure; total = today's
    real total + the sum of the effects (owner, 09-18, reconfirmed). Effect = (target - START) x basis (cost rows
    inverted). Basis = COVERED hours, never seat hours; materials basis = COGS / 100.
R4  Display: keep TODAY fixed, show MODELLED beside it and the CHANGE (Q1).
R5  Overhead levers touch only the UNDISTRIBUTED General Overheads pool, line by line - never a line already
    distributed to an asset (that would corrupt Cost Allocation).
R6  Labour levers are per staff type; asset levers per unit. An asset-driven unit is ONE lever - the all-in unit rate;
    its labour rows show "in_unit_rate", not leverable; the machine share is shown live via Rate Builder's own
    calculate_net_asset_rate. The all-in guide is not a cap.
R7  Materials: START 0%, FLOOR 0%, CEILING = Rate Builder markup (View A floors materials; owner D-VIEW).
R8  Shortfall distribution stays PRO-RATA (order-independent), not a waterfall.
R9  Row labels are "unit / staff type" (name trap: working unit "Site Crew" vs staff type "Site Crew").

4. INPUTS AND STORAGE
===============================================================================

only as a cross-check, it is inert for additive levers) and useGeneralOverheads().card.summary.grouped_overhead_rows
(read-only; mounting it runs General Overheads' own hydrate / save / P&L-sync effects, guarded).
Storage: qs_tools_business_modelling_levers_v1 (lazy useState read). Old "assets:<id>" levers are pruned
automatically for asset-driven units. Legacy baseline snapshots (old engine) keep pre-F12 hours until re-locked
(Labour F17, Summaries S-H4).

 1. [OWNER DECISION] Two kinds of change in Model mode. A LEVER changes a number that already exists (sale price per unit, buy price per unit, overhead percentage, charge-out rate): ordinary modelling, not a scenario. A SCENARIO adds or replaces something that does not exist yet (a truck, a driver on PAYE, a person, a digger). A new PLANT is a different design, later. Sale and buy price levers in units mode need units mode first; a COGS lever widens Tier 1 and needs the owner's explicit yes (Addendum 38 items 1 and 2 stand).
 2. [OWNER DECISION] Buying a truck is a scenario with an ADD or REPLACE choice. ADD: more capacity, thirteen trucks not twelve; nothing is switched off and nothing is paid twice. REPLACE: a hired-in cost line comes off the working group and an owned asset plus a driver on PAYE goes on, so the scenario must retire the hired line at the same moment or the truck is paid twice (same retire-the-placeholder idea as Addendum 37 item 12). Hired is a variable cost (paid only when it runs); owned is a fixed cost (finance, insurance, wage whether it runs or not), so owning costs significantly more and needs real volume. The owner is mainly interested in REPLACE. [FINDING] Scenario adds of staff and assets already exist (Addenda 30 to 32); retiring a hired cost line and add-or-replace are NEW. Units mode is needed first for the cost-of-sales side.
 3. [OWNER DECISION, HEADLINE FEATURE] The "WHEN TO GROW" number (owner's working name; technical name: affordability threshold). For a proposed purchase (a staff member, a digger, a truck, a bowl and a permanent driver, even a non-productive 150,000 ute) it answers: how much does the WHOLE BUSINESS need to be doing, on a consistent basis, before this purchase is safe? It is a budget and timing question. Method (arithmetic only): the existing fixed cost plus the new fixed cost, divided by the margin per unit (cubes in units mode; hours or revenue in hours mode), gives the volume the whole business needs; compare it with what the business does now; the gap is the safety margin. It is NOT "how many extra cubes the new truck must carry" (the first framing in chat, rejected by the owner), although the extra-volume view could be a second display. The owner called it the "wow" or epiphany number: an owner with no CFO or analyst cannot work it out by hand, because it mixes finance, wages, overhead and volume at once.
 4. [OPEN] What "safe" means: bare break-even, or break-even plus a profit the owner wants to keep. Recommend showing both as labelled figures, owner decides. "Consistently" needs a period basis (a volume held over time); the first version can only compare with the P&L-derived volume, and the live layer (invoice lines) is what would measure consistency.
 5. [DECISION, SEQUENCING] It works in BOTH modes (hours mode could already do it with existing scenario adds), but it is parked: the core comes first and no new features are added before the core is built (owner). It is logged here with the proviso that it belongs to the units mode sequence; the units version waits for units mode (R4).
 6. [DESIGN RULES] Highlight it prominently in the UI as the payoff of the chain, not buried in a drill-down. A new calculated figure needs a trace node matching the screen and a calc map entry before it counts as done (standing rule, Addendum 37 item 3). Wording is arithmetic only, no "should", "need to", "consider" (Addendum 37 item 6; AI_LAYER advice rule: show options, never "you should"). The number is only as good as its inputs and the margin per unit can be thin (about 10 a cube in the test case), so small errors in buy price or truck rate move it a lot: do not show it to anyone until the chain under it has been proven on real numbers.
