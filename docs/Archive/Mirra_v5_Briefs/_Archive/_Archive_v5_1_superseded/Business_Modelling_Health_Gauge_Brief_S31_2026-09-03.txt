# 1. PURPOSE

This is the single source of truth for finishing Business Modelling and
building the health gauge. It consolidates every decision made in the
2026-09-02/03 voice-review session and locks the build order.

Core principle carried through every section below: every cost source
should be tested on whether it pays for itself, independently, with
zero cross-subsidy. The tool observes and explains. It never decides
for the user. Nothing here forbids a deliberate loss-leader - it only
refuses to let one happen invisibly.

# 2. CASCADE RETIREMENT (CONFIRMED, FINAL)

The existing cascade logic in
`hooks/useBusinessOutcomePerSourceRevenue.js` (`apply_real_capacity` -
simultaneous-% Phase 1, revenue-share Phase 2) is RETIRED from Business
Modelling entirely. It continues to power Business Outcome's smoothed /
blended "current reality" view only.

Business Modelling's per-source target is purely independent:

  target_revenue (per source) = source's own true_cost
  required_rate_delta = (true_cost - modelled_revenue) / source's own
                         driver quantity

No source's target ever references another source's numbers. This is
final - not a proposal.

# 4. RANKING PRINCIPLE FOR FUTURE LEVER WORK

When more than one lever exists, prioritise by NET PROFIT IMPACT, not
raw dollar size of the cost line. A large cost line does not
automatically mean a large available fix lives there - it means fixing
it delivers a large swing per dollar of adjustment. Materials is built
first not because it is assumed to be the biggest win, but because it
is currently invisible to any net-profit ranking at all - it has no
lever to rank.

# 5. MATERIALS / COG LEVER

- User's mental model, confirmed consistent with existing brief
  language (13_MIRRA_REVENUE_COGS and 30_MIRRA_BUSINESS_MODELLING v4.0
  LOCKED briefs), but NOT YET CODE-VERIFIED: markup-on-cost, a
  percentage added on top of what was paid, not margin-on-price.
- System rule (must hold): there is ONE blended material markup at the
  model level. Do not invent fake supplier-level or category-level
  markups. Supplier/category rows may show contribution/influence to
  the one blended result only, never a fabricated per-supplier margin.
- Mode-dependent COGS rule to respect:
  - Hours-based/service mode: Revenue - COGS = Material Margin.
    Materials are observational, not part of hourly recovery burden.
  - Product/unit mode: Revenue - COGS = Margin Pool. COGS is
    operational and tested against unit cost burden.
- Existing brief-level formula (unverified in code, verify in Phase 1
  step 1 before building):
    category_adjustment_amount = category_spend x adjustment_percent
    modelled_total_cog = current_total_cog + category_adjustment_amount
    modelled_margin_pool = total_revenue - modelled_total_cog
    modelled_gross_margin_percent = modelled_margin_pool / total_revenue
- Materials gets a breakeven-style default the same way revenue levers
  do (Section 8): the lever pre-fills at the calculated point where
  Materials nets to zero on its own, fully editable.

# 6. PER-GROUP DRIVER UNIT (NOT HOURS EVERYWHERE)

Confirmed: each operating group already has its real driver unit
defined upstream (Cost Allocation / Rate Builder / Assets utilisation
setup). Examples discussed: two-inch line pump -> cubic metres, digger
-> hours, concrete laying -> square metres.

Requirement: the Business Modelling lever for each group must read and
display in that group's real unit, and the underlying breakeven
divide-by must use the correct driver quantity per group (true_cost /
group's own driver quantity), not a hardcoded hours assumption.

Sub-requirement (from Rate Builder discussion): confirm Rate Builder
genuinely blends each/hourly/per-unit component charges (e.g. line
pump's set-up fee (each) + hourly pump charge + cubic-metre concrete
rate) into one job total, and correctly derives the group's headline
effective rate in its chosen unit from that blend. This must be
checked in code (Phase 1, step 2) before the lever is built on top of
it.

Locked constraint: the lever moves PRICE only. It never adjusts the
fixed utilisation/driver quantity that Assets already defines (e.g.
expected annual hours for the pump). Utilisation as its own separate
lever is deferred (Section 8.1).

# 7. ACCORDION UI (BOTH PAGES, IDENTICAL PATTERN)

Business Modelling currently has two separate cards: one carried over
from Business Outcome showing numbers, and a second card for entering
each individual rate. These merge into ONE card.

Behaviour:
- Click a source's line (e.g. "Materials / COG") -> that line expands
  in place showing the rate/markup input and its own breakeven detail.
- Any other expanded line collapses automatically - only one rate
  entry is open at a time.
- Totals at the top of the page update live as the value is typed.
- No separate second card/table to scan for the matching row.

Apply the SAME accordion pattern to Business Outcome: clicking a line
there expands outcome detail in place (not a rate input, since Outcome
is read-only), with the same collapse/expand behaviour. Goal stated by
user: both pages should operate exactly the same so the system feels
like one tool, not two bolted together.

# 8. FUTURE LEVER FAMILY (LOGGED, NOT BUILT NOW)

Six levers were identified as the complete family for this business
type. Three are in this build (rate/per-group breakeven, materials
markup - Sections 5-6). Three are deferred:

## 8.1 Utilisation (per asset/group)
- Second lever alongside price. Raises/lowers the annual
  hours/quantity a group's asset actually runs.
- NOT free upside: variable costs (confirmed examples - fuel, the
  associated labour hours) scale with utilisation; fixed costs
  (insurance, registration, finance, depreciation) do not.
- Decision taken: do NOT ask the user to classify every cost line as
  fixed/variable (too much extra input, against the "don't add
  complexity" instinct). Default: treat the whole asset annual cost as
  FIXED, no split. This slightly understates true upside (fuel really
  would rise a little) but never overstates it, and costs zero extra
  input - consistent with the defensible-over-precise standard.
- Labour lock rule: an asset's associated labour hours scale in
  lockstep, one-to-one, with that asset's utilisation hours when the
  asset genuinely requires an operator to run (e.g. the pump cannot
  run without its operator). This is a real, defensible mechanical
  link, not an estimate.
- Stacking: rate and utilisation can both move at once and should
  compound, not be mutually exclusive.

## 8.2 Non-productive asset / labour restructure
- Distinct from "cut overhead by a percentage." This is a structural
  swap: dispose of a non-productive asset (e.g. a ute used only for
  commuting, on finance, insured, but generating zero revenue) and
  replace its ownership cost with a smaller variable cost (fuel
  card/allowance). Same logic applies to non-productive staff/roles
  that don't generate revenue against any operating group.
- More a binary toggle/decision test ("keep as is" vs "remove/
  restructure") than a continuous slider.
- IMPORTANT SEQUENCING NOTE: because this changes the cost POOL itself
  (not just revenue/margin), it should generally be evaluated BEFORE
  fine-tuning rate and utilisation levers when a user works through the
  page, otherwise rate targets calculated first become stale the
  moment a non-productive cost is removed afterward, forcing rework.
  The tool must NOT enforce this order rigidly - the page stays a free
  sandbox the user can move through in any direction - but the
  suggested default walkthrough order should surface this insight.

## 8.3 General overheads adjustment - LINE BY LINE, NOT ONE PERCENTAGE
- CONFIRMED DECISION: reject a single blanket "adjust overheads by X%"
  lever. Overhead pools mix genuinely different flexibility tiers:
  - LOCKED (never editable): statutory employer costs, e.g. employer
    KiwiSaver contributions. Not negotiable, full stop.
  - HEAVILY FLAGGED (editable but must warn): mandatory-but-technically
    -changeable items, e.g. ACC or ACC-alternative cover providers.
    Editable, but the UI must warn clearly that this is mandatory
    cover and changing the number does not mean the obligation goes
    away.
  - FREELY EDITABLE: genuinely discretionary costs, e.g. insurance
    premiums, software subscriptions. These can be renegotiated,
    switched, or cancelled.
- Each overhead line item needs its own adjustment input and a
  lock/flag status, not one shared slider.
- Two distinct overhead homes to check/handle:
  1. Overhead already ATTRIBUTED to specific assets (e.g. fuel,
     insurance pushed down into each asset's own cost via Cost
     Allocation) - a real-world win (e.g. a renegotiated insurance
     premium) must flow through and reduce cost on every asset
     carrying a slice of it.
  2. The REMAINING general pool, spread proportionately across
     operating groups.
  A single category-level change (e.g. "insurance renegotiated") must
  be able to correctly reduce BOTH homes at once, not just the general
  pool. CHECK BEFORE BUILD: confirm how Assets / Cost Allocation /
  General Overheads currently track the attributed-vs-remaining split
  before writing this lever.
- Same locked/flagged logic applies to labour-attributed overhead
  items (e.g. ACC, employer contributions locked; some other
  labour-attributed line items may be discretionary).

## 8.4 Labour productivity (named, not yet scoped)
- Productive hours actually delivered vs paid hours, distinct from
  utilisation (utilisation is the asset's clock; productivity is the
  efficiency of the person within their hours).

# 9. HEALTH GAUGE (FULLY SPECIFIED - REPLACES S30 SECTION 12)

## 9.1 What the gauge is NOT
It is NOT derived from the retired cascade logic. It does NOT reuse
Business Outcome's smoothed cross-subsidy math as its calculation
engine.

## 9.2 The metric (single source of truth)
The gauge shows the ratio between two totals:

  1. SMOOTHED target revenue - the revenue needed for the whole
     business to net to zero, cross-subsidy allowed (Business
     Outcome's existing job, already built and verified).
  2. INDEPENDENT target revenue - the revenue needed for EVERY single
     source, including Materials, to net to zero entirely on its own,
     zero cross-subsidy (the Phase 2-3 work in Section 3 of this
     brief).

  health_ratio = smoothed_target_revenue / independent_target_revenue

Ratio close to 1 = healthy, needle sits toward green (real profit,
minimal hidden subsidy). Large gap = mostly cross-subsidised, needle
sits toward red (the good-looking blended number is mostly borrowed
from strong sources propping up weak ones).

This is a HARD BLOCKER: the gauge cannot be built with a real number
until Phase 2 (Materials independent target) and Phase 3 (per-group
independent target wired as visible) are both complete. Do not build
a placeholder/rough version and redefine its metric later.

## 9.3 Visual design (confirmed)
- Semi-circle arc gauge, red -> amber -> green gradient across the
  arc.
- Needle(s) pointing to position on the arc.
- Plain-language caption below the gauge, e.g. "X of Y sources
  standing on their own."
- NO tab switcher between Baseline/Modelling/Live views (an earlier
  mockup used tabs - explicitly rejected).

## 9.4 Needle count per page (confirmed, final)
- Business Outcome page: ONE gauge, ONE needle - "Baseline" (today's
  real smoothed state). No other needles on this page.
- Business Modelling page: ONE gauge, TWO needles simultaneously
  visible on the same arc - Baseline needle (fixed) + Modelling needle
  (moves live as the user adjusts any lever on the page). Not two
  separate gauges side by side - one gauge, two needles.
- Future Dashboard (Section 11, separate build): the SAME gauge
  pattern gains a THIRD needle - "Live" - continuously updating from
  real, persisted actuals data, distinct from the one-off Modelling
  scenario needle. This is additive (grows from 1 -> 2 -> 3 needles as
  each page is built), not a rebuild each time.

## 9.5 Breakeven anchor
Breakeven (every source independently at zero net position) is the
mathematical anchor the gauge orbits around - it is the point where
smoothed_target and independent_target are equal, i.e. health_ratio =
1, full green. This ties directly to the breakeven-default behaviour
in Section 10.

# 10. BREAKEVEN DEFAULTS (LEVER PRE-FILL RULE)

Applies only to REVENUE-side levers - rate/price, materials markup,
(future) utilisation. Each of these has an actual equation that solves
to zero, so the system pre-fills the lever's input with the calculated
value that gets that source to independent breakeven. This is a
neutral, guided starting point only - every field remains fully
user-editable, and the system never tells the user what to charge.

Does NOT apply to COST-POOL levers - non-productive assets/labour,
general overheads (Section 8.2, 8.3). These have no revenue driver to
solve to zero against, so they default to TODAY'S REAL FIGURE, and the
lever lets the user test deviations from current reality rather than
deviations from a calculated ideal.

# 11. DASHBOARD (SEPARATE FUTURE BUILD - LOGGED HERE FOR CONTINUITY)

Not part of this brief's active build scope (gated behind Phases 1-5
plus a database build), but the shape is decided enough to log now so
nothing is lost.

## 11.1 Trigger for the database build
The database/storage layer is deliberately built LATE in the overall
system sequence, after calculation logic is stable, so schema doesn't
get rebuilt every time the model changes underneath it. The live
Dashboard is the first feature that genuinely forces a database to
exist - it needs persisted, accumulating month-by-month actuals to
read from, not just numbers calculated fresh in memory per page load.
Database build should immediately follow Business Modelling
completion (Phases 1-5 above), ahead of the Dashboard itself.

## 11.2 Storage shape (confirmed analogy: one workbook, separate sheets)
Same storage layer, structurally distinct record types/tables:
- BASELINE sheet/table - locked snapshot(s), never silently
  overwritten. Represents a fixed belief at a point in time.
- MODELLING sheet/table - locked scenario snapshot(s) the user has
  saved, same never-overwritten rule.
- LIVE ACTUALS sheet/table - a genuinely different shape: an
  accumulating, appending record, one new row added per month,
  designed to be summed/trended up to a yearly view. Actuals create
  variance against baseline/modelling - they do not rewrite history.
The Dashboard itself stores nothing new - it is a live read/blend
across the three tables above, not a fourth data store.

## 11.3 Monthly re-run mechanics (validated, with honest caveats)
Confirmed: in the steady state (stable Xero categories, stable Assets
and Labour inputs, only P&L amounts changing month to month, expected
to be true ~95% of the time), importing a new month's P&L + Revenue/
COGS data is genuinely close to "import file, whole chain
recalculates itself" - because every module's contract and formula was
already proven correct against the first dataset. This is the payoff
of the classification/module-boundary discipline already built.

Caveats (do not treat as automatically solved by "the math already
works"):
- New/never-seen category or account lines must surface via the
  EXISTING "review required / not assigned" mechanism - flag ONLY the
  new unassigned line, do not re-surface or re-flag categories that
  are already correctly mapped.
- Non-P&L actuals (productive hours, utilisation, headcount changes,
  pay rises) are a separate, deliberately out-of-scope input stream
  for this phase (explicitly parked by user - "let's not go down that
  path just yet"), and must feed in alongside the P&L import, not be
  assumed to update automatically from it.
- Readiness/reconciliation gating must define behaviour for a month
  that fails gating on import (block silently vs flag vs freeze last
  good baseline) - not yet decided, needs its own small design pass
  when this phase starts.

## 11.4 Dashboard content (five items, confirmed shape)
1. The gauge - three-needle version, centrepiece.
2. Net position trend - live actual net position plotted against what
   baseline predicted and what the model promised, over time, not just
   a single snapshot.
3. Per-source live scoreboard - the accordion list pattern (Section 7)
   showing LIVE actuals instead of scenario inputs: which sources are
   currently paying for themselves, which are being quietly
   subsidised again even after a fix was modelled.
4. Biggest current live gap - whichever source has the largest live
   shortfall against its independent target right now (dynamic, not a
   static list).
5. Drift-from-model alert - flags when live reality has meaningfully
   diverged from what was modelled/committed to.

CONFIRMED REFINEMENT: the dashboard must show BOTH directions, not
just shortfall/red. A source tracking above its independent target
should be shown with equal visual weight as good news, not just
absence of a red flag. The gauge's full red-amber-green arc already
supports this; the scoreboard and biggest-gap cards must be designed
the same way - a genuine two-sided view, not a list of problems.

Deferred from this list: labour/asset utilisation as its own dashboard
number - genuinely tied to real invoicing/job actuals, which is
further down the track than this phase.

## 11.5 Quote Checker - explicitly out of this chain
Quote Checker validates a specific quote against Rate Builder/the
model. It is optional and business-type-dependent (irrelevant to a
business that doesn't quote jobs, e.g. retail/subscription models). It
consumes outputs but nothing upstream depends on it. It does NOT block
or gate the Dashboard build and should not be treated as a required
step in this core chain. Future briefs should formalise it as a
conditional module outside the Outcome -> Modelling -> Dashboard
chain.

# 16. FINAL PRINCIPLE

Business Modelling and the gauge exist to make invisible cross-subsidy
visible, source by source, and to let the business owner decide - with
full information, not a hidden blend - which parts of the business
should genuinely pay for themselves and which they are choosing to
subsidise on purpose. The tool never makes that decision for them.
