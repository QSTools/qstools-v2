2. TWO FAILED BUSINESS MODELLING ATTEMPTS TODAY - WHY, PRECISELY
===============================================================================

**Attempt 1 - real-capacity cascade rerun via View B (built, tested,
then reverted same day).** useBusinessOutcomePerSourceRevenue's
override mechanism (built 2026-09-17: labour_charge_out_rate_overrides,
asset_blended_rate_overrides, materials_markup_percent_override) was
wired into the lever card's live_headline via a new
view_b_headline_real_capacity selector field. THE FLAW, discovered
live: View B's real-capacity cascade is DESIGNED to always reconcile
its total to today's real revenue minus real cost, regardless of any
lever input - the total literally cannot move no matter what rate or
markup is changed, because the cascade mathematically redistributes
margin to always land on the real total. Confirmed live: changing
materials markup moved WHICH sources were "being carried" but never
moved the total itself. This defeats the entire purpose of a
forward-looking modelling tool. REVERTED same day - see
hooks/useBusinessModelling.js's own comments, live_headline now reads
buildLiveLeverHeadline again (the older, independent, uncapped
engine), NOT buildRealEngineLeverHeadline (deleted from use, function
itself still exists in businessModellingLeverSelectors.js, unused).

**Attempt 2 - the pre-existing independent engine (buildLiveLeverHeadline)
- also confirmed inadequate, different reason.** This engine sums
every working unit's OWN full, uncapped, independent claim (rate x
assumed hours, no cross-subsidy) - materials' own input
(build_materials_input) was ALREADY correctly fixed earlier in the
session to use Rate Builder's real markup formula, so this was never
actually broken for materials specifically. But live-tested with real
numbers (COG at 10% markup): produced a total around $862,452 - a real,
mathematically consistent number (traced and confirmed correct
arithmetic), but essentially meaningless for "what does my real
business look like if I make this one change" - it's dominated by
every OTHER untouched working unit's own full assumed billing ceiling
stacked together (e.g. PC15 alone claiming ~$295k independently),
nothing to do with today's real, cross-subsidized business.

**CONCLUSION FROM BOTH FAILURES:** neither "pinned to today's real
total no matter what" (Attempt 1) nor "every untouched source jumps to
its own independent fantasy ceiling" (Attempt 2) is the right
behaviour for a lever. The right behaviour, agreed live with the user:
touch ONE lever, recalculate ONLY that one working unit using its real
cost and the new rate, and leave EVERY OTHER working unit exactly at
its real, today's achieved figure - untouched. Total net profit then
= today's real total, plus exactly the effect of the one thing
changed. Not yet built - this is the mechanism the "full circle"
architecture in Section 3 needs to support, not a separate patch.

3. THE NEW DIRECTION - "FULL CIRCLE" ARCHITECTURE (agreed, not built)
===============================================================================

User's own description, the real target architecture, worth quoting
directly rather than paraphrasing since precision matters here:

"we are going to bring in the overheads from Cost Summary - these are
what remains after distribution to assets and labour has been done...
when I say distributed I mean that from the P&L we've said fuel
belongs to running our vehicles, so use that so we can work out the
true vehicle cost - that gets pulled out of general overheads for
Cost Summary... this applies to insurance, finance etc. Labour works
the same - these all belong to labour so we can see what our min
recoverable rate is for every staff member/type - this is also pulled
out and potentially adjusted with an ACC calc and employer
contributions. So Cost Summary overheads are what's left of that pool...
that remaining overhead pool is what gets distributed later as a
portion... now we know the full min recoverable rate for each labour
and asset item. Then we have built Cost Allocation and Rate Builder
and Outcome which tests your assumptions against the actual business
and creates the achieved rate. So if we reduce overheads that are left
in Cost Summary via modelling we change all the downstream min
recovery rates, and then if we change the labour rates that changes
the asset rates - and that completes the circle."

**In plain terms:** real injection points at EVERY real decision point
in the chain (Cost Summary's remaining overhead, Labour's rates/on-costs,
Rate Builder's working-unit pricing), each one's edited value flowing
FORWARD through the same real calculation chain that already runs
today with real data - Cost Allocation, Rate Builder, Business Outcome
- rather than overriding only the final output (which is what both
failed attempts today tried, from opposite ends of "how much should
be uncapped").

**Important nuance, not yet confirmed either way:** rate-lever changes
specifically (e.g. "raise the Operator's chargeout rate, see PC55's
achieved rate respond") may already be MOSTLY built - the existing
override mechanism (labour_charge_out_rate_overrides,
asset_blended_rate_overrides) sits at exactly this point in the chain
already. What's likely still needed there is just the Section 2
"only touch what changed" cascade fix, not new injection points
reaching back into Rate Builder/Labour themselves. The GENUINELY new,
unbuilt piece is overhead cost - "reduce this Cost Summary line, see
the effect ripple down" has ZERO lever support anywhere currently.
Do not assume this without checking - see Section 4.
