1. THE RESOLUTION - WORKED THROUGH LIVE AGAINST REAL DATA
===============================================================================

**THE ANSWER, IN THE USER'S OWN FINAL WORDS:** "Each part on its own is
the best case upside of what you have specified in the modules that
you are charging for rates and hrs." Confirmed correct.

**What this actually means, precisely:** "Each Part On Its Own" is not
a measurement of reality, and was never meant to compete with the real
headline ("How the Business Runs"). It answers: if every source
achieved its FULL assumed billable hours at its CURRENTLY SET rate,
with zero cap to real revenue and zero cross-subsidy, what's the best
possible outcome the numbers currently entered in the system would
produce? A source that STILL can't cover its own real cost even under
this best-case, no-help scenario has a genuine, structural rate/hours
problem - isolated from every other noise (materials, cross-subsidy,
real revenue shortfall). That's the real diagnostic value.

**The original open question (preserved in full below, Section 3)
started from a real, live discrepancy:** three independently-built
figures agreed exactly (-$110,204: "How the Business Runs", View B,
Business Modelling's real headline), while "Each Part On Its Own"
showed a wildly different number. That gap was fully explained, not
dismissed - see Section 1 above and the full reasoning trail below.

2. A REAL, SEPARATE BUG WAS FOUND AND FIXED ALONG THE WAY
===============================================================================

Not part of the original question, but discovered while investigating
it: this view's Materials figure was using the View A REVENUE RESIDUAL
(total_revenue - labour_modelled - asset_modelled) instead of Rate
Builder's own real markup-based revenue - the exact same broken
mechanism already identified and fixed for Business Modelling's
independent lever engine earlier this same session
(build_materials_input, businessModellingLeverSelectors.js). Since this
view's labour/asset revenue is uncapped and already exceeds real
revenue by design, Materials was absorbing that entire gap as a fake,
un-earned loss (showing -$960,245 against Rate Builder's own real
+$186,014).

FIXED in build_naive_headline() (BusinessOutcomePerSourceRevenueCard.jsx) -
now reads per_source.view_b?.materials?.modelled_revenue (Rate
Builder's real markup revenue), falling back to the old residual only
if genuinely unavailable. Live-verified: Materials now correctly shows
+$186,014, matching Rate Builder exactly.
