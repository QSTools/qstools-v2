# QS Tools — Business Plan & Growth Destination Brief

## 1. Purpose

QS Tools is a financial control platform for businesses that need to understand whether their pricing, cost recovery, job performance, and business model actually work.

The tool is not just an accounting add-on, job costing tool, labour-rate calculator, or dashboard.

It is designed to sit between:

* business operations
* quoting/pricing
* purchasing
* invoices
* accounting software
* management decisions

Its core purpose is to mirror the business back to the owner in a structured way so they can see:

* what the business costs
* how those costs must be recovered
* whether the operational structure can deliver that recovery
* whether jobs, invoices, labour, materials, and margins are drifting away from the plan
* what needs to change next

The long-term destination is a financial control platform that gives business owners a live view of whether the business is working, not just a historical accounting result after the fact.

---

## 2. Core Market Problem

Most small and medium businesses do not really know if their pricing model works.

They often rely on:

* end-of-year P&L reports
* gut feel
* accountant summaries
* job-by-job intuition
* spreadsheets
* disconnected quoting and invoicing systems

The problem is that by the time the P&L shows the result, the damage has already happened.

Common issues include:

* labour being under-recovered
* material margins hiding labour losses
* supplier price drift not being reflected in quotes
* actual quantities exceeding quoted quantities
* overheads not being properly recovered
* invoices not being reconciled against purchase expectations
* staff and asset structure not matching the pricing model
* business owners not knowing which part of the model is failing

QS Tools solves this by turning business data into a live control system.

---

## 3. Core Product Position

QS Tools should be positioned as:

**A financial control platform that connects cost, recovery, structure, and actual trading activity into one decision system.**

The simplest description:

**QS Tools shows whether your business is priced, structured, and operating in a way that can actually make money.**

The core language should remain:

* What does the business cost?
* How must that cost be recovered?
* Can the structure deliver it?
* Should you keep running this model?
* Where is the business drifting?

---

## 4. System Architecture Direction

The current architecture already supports the long-term business vision.

The system should continue to follow this commercial flow:

1. **Input Modules**

   * Labour
   * Employee Overheads
   * Assets
   * General Overheads
   * Revenue / COGS inputs later

2. **Cost Summary**

   * Defines what the business costs
   * Aggregates labour, people cost, assets, and overheads
   * Produces total cost burden and required recovery baseline

3. **Recovery Summary**

   * Defines how the cost is expected to be recovered
   * Labour recovery
   * Asset recovery
   * Overhead absorption

4. **Cost Allocation**

   * Defines whether the business structure can deliver the recovery model
   * Staff-to-asset capability
   * Operational groups
   * Structural readiness

5. **Recovery Outcome**

   * Converts cost, recovery, and structure into a business decision
   * Viable
   * Marginal
   * Not viable

6. **Budget / Business Plan**

   * Converts the model into a forward business plan
   * Revenue
   * COGS
   * Operating expenses
   * Net profit
   * Growth scenario

7. **Quote / Job Validation**

   * Checks quotes and jobs against the business model
   * Labour hours
   * materials
   * subcontractors
   * expected margin
   * required recovery

8. **Invoice / Actuals Layer**

   * Captures actual invoice and cost data
   * Compares actuals to quote, PO, budget, and recovery model
   * Finds drift

9. **Feedback Loop**

   * Uses accepted jobs and actual invoice data to update future pricing intelligence
   * Shows whether pricing assumptions are still valid

## 5. Revenue Model

The core revenue model should remain simple.

### Primary Revenue Stream 1 — Subscription

The subscription is the entry point.

Purpose:

* gives user access to the platform
* stores their business model
* provides dashboards and decision outputs
* creates recurring base revenue

Likely early pricing:

* low-friction entry around $25–$50/month
* higher tiers later as the product matures

The subscription should not be expected to carry the full business model on its own.

It is the gateway.

---

### Primary Revenue Stream 2 — Invoice Automation

Invoice automation is the main scalable revenue engine.

This is the strongest revenue stream because it is:

* recurring
* usage-based
* tied to customer activity
* operationally sticky
* valuable because it saves manual processing time
* strategically important because it feeds actual cost data into the system

The user pays because the tool removes the pain of:

* manual invoice extraction
* messy Power Automate workflows
* Excel table cleanup
* inconsistent supplier descriptions
* manual Power BI preparation
* invoice-to-PO checking
* data cleaning before analysis

This stream can become larger than the subscription.

Example pricing options:

* included invoice volume per tier
* overage per invoice
* processing bundles
* monthly invoice automation package

The model should avoid pure micro-pricing that makes users nervous. Bundled usage with fair overage is cleaner.

---

### Secondary Revenue Stream — Advanced Data Access / Power BI Connector

This is a legitimate advanced revenue stream, but not for all users.

It should be positioned as:

**Advanced Data Access**

Not just:

**Power BI connector**

What is being sold:

* clean, governed data model access
* curated reporting views
* data warehouse access
* read-only export layer
* Power BI / Tableau / analytics connection

This is valuable for:

* larger contractors
* accountants
* advisors
* internal analysts
* businesses already using Power BI
* users who want custom KPIs beyond the built-in dashboard

This should be an advanced-tier feature.

Conservative pricing:

* $50–$100/month add-on
* or included in an Advanced plan

Uptake should be modelled conservatively, around 3–5% of users initially.

This is not a mass-market feature, but it is a credible additional revenue stream.

---

### Supporting Revenue Stream — Benchmarking

Benchmarking should not be treated as a major revenue driver.

Static benchmark data is not worth much as a monthly product because once the user knows the range, the value drops.

Benchmarking should be treated as:

* a trust layer
* onboarding support
* small add-on
* higher-tier feature
* industry reference tool

Conservative pricing:

* $0 included in higher tiers
* $5/month add-on
* one-off benchmark pack later

It should not distract from subscription and invoice automation.

---

### Supporting Revenue Stream — Structured Onboarding

Onboarding can be charged, but it must not become custom consulting chaos.

Acceptable:

* fixed setup package
* fixed-scope implementation
* template-based onboarding
* guided setup call

Avoid:

* bespoke builds
* custom reporting
* custom logic
* one-off enterprise work too early

Potential pricing:

* $250–$1,000 setup depending on customer size

---

## 6. What Not To Build Too Early

Avoid these early:

* payment processing
* supplier payment handling
* custom enterprise installs
* bespoke dashboards
* deep benchmarking monetisation
* complex marketplace features
* large bespoke integrations
* on-premise deployments
* consulting-heavy implementation

These may create distraction, liability, support burden, or custom-scope problems.

The product should remain repeatable.

---

## 7. Growth Destination

The long-term destination is a global financial control platform for SMEs and trade/service/product businesses.

The core model is universal:

* every business has revenue
* every business has costs
* every business has margin
* every business has actual vs expected drift
* every business needs to know whether the model works

The first niche should be narrow enough to prove the model.

Likely first beachhead:

* NZ construction / trade / subcontractor businesses
* labour + materials businesses
* businesses with quote, PO, invoice and margin pain

Then expand to:

* Australian trades and contractors
* UK/US contractors
* service businesses
* manufacturing/light production
* product-based businesses
* retail/wholesale later

The core engine should remain the same, with different industry wrappers and onboarding flows.

## 9. Strategic Revenue Priorities

Priority order:

1. Subscription
2. Invoice automation
3. Quote/job validation
4. Advanced data access / Power BI connector
5. Structured onboarding
6. Benchmarking as support layer
7. Advisor/accountant channel later

Do not overcomplicate the business model.

The strongest model is:

**low-friction subscription + scalable invoice automation + advanced add-ons for power users.**

---

## 10. Ultimate Outcome

QS Tools can become a global financial control platform if it stays focused on the central promise:

**Show the business owner whether the business is actually working, where it is drifting, and what needs to change.**

The product should not try to replace Xero or accounting software.

It should become the layer that makes accounting data operationally useful before, during, and after business activity.

The destination is:

* business plan
* quote/pricing control
* PO/invoice actuals
* margin/recovery drift
* decision output
* future pricing correction

That is the closed loop.
