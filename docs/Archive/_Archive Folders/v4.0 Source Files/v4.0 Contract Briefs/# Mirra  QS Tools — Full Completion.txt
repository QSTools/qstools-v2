# Mirra / QS Tools — Full Completion Brief

## The Completed Closed-Loop Business Recovery Tool

## 1. Executive Summary

Mirra is a closed-loop business recovery system.

On completion, it will help a business owner understand whether their business model is working, whether their quotes are safe, whether their jobs are proving the model, and whether monthly financial performance is improving or drifting away from target.

Mirra will connect:

```txt
Accounting truth
↓
Business recovery model
↓
Required rates
↓
Quote checking
↓
Works orders
↓
Purchase orders
↓
Labour / time tracking
↓
Asset usage
↓
Supplier invoices
↓
Job actuals
↓
Reconciliation
↓
Xero
↓
Monthly P&L
↓
Model feedback
↓
Approved model improvement
↓
Future quote targets
```

This loop is the product.

Mirra is not just a dashboard, quoting tool, job costing tool, estimating tool, accounting add-on, or project management system.

It is a business model control system.

It turns accounting data into recovery targets, turns those targets into rates and quote rules, checks quotes before work starts, tracks actual job delivery, reconciles job reality back to accounting, and uses the next P&L to prove whether the model is working.

The current interface is complex because the engine is being built in full. The completed user interface will be much simpler.

The final user experience should follow this principle:

```txt
Answer first.
Reason second.
Evidence third.
Machinery only on drilldown.
```

The system underneath can remain complex because that complexity is what creates trusted answers.

The user should see the simple truth first.

---

# 2. Core Product Statement

Mirra answers:

```txt
Is the business model working?
Are quotes aligned to that model?
Are jobs proving or disproving the model?
Is monthly P&L performance improving?
What needs to change next?
```

Most systems answer only part of this:

```txt
What does the P&L say?
Did this job make money?
What is the quote value?
What are the job actuals?
What is the current margin?
```

Mirra asks the deeper question:

```txt
Does every job, quote, invoice, labour hour, asset hour, supplier cost, and monthly P&L result align with the business recovery model?
```

That is the completed product.

# 4. What Mirra Will Do on Completion

## 4.1 Import and preserve accounting truth

Mirra will import accounting data from Xero, Excel, CSV, manual import, or future API sources.

It will preserve:

* original account names
* original supplier names
* source file metadata
* source period
* signed values
* GST/tax where relevant
* raw and normalised names
* source trace
* import history

Xero remains the accounting source of truth.

Mirra does not replace Xero.

Mirra uses Xero and P&L data to build and test the business model.

---

## 4.2 Build the business operating model from the P&L

Mirra will use the P&L as the accounting anchor.

It will classify and analyse:

* revenue
* COGS / direct costs
* labour
* assets
* general overheads
* finance / interest
* other income
* net profit
* accounting benchmark totals

It will then bridge the P&L to Mirra operating truth.

The user should be able to see:

```txt
What the P&L says
What Mirra says
Why they differ
Which module caused the difference
Whether the difference is classification, timing, accounting treatment, or operating model treatment
```

The system will not hide differences.

It will explain them.

---

## 4.3 Separate macro business baseline from micro recovery strategy

Mirra will clearly separate the business operating baseline from the recovery strategy.

## Macro layer

The macro layer answers:

```txt
What does the business cost to be open?
Does the trading margin pool cover the business cost?
What does the business cost per open hour?
```

It uses business opening hours and total operating cost.

## Micro layer

The micro layer answers:

```txt
How will the business recover that cost?
Which driver carries the burden?
How do labour, assets, materials, overheads, units, and operating groups recover cost?
```

It may use:

* productive labour hours
* selected recovery hours
* asset utilisation hours
* unit drivers
* group drivers
* product drivers

This prevents the system from confusing:

```txt
business availability
productive labour capacity
selected recovery strategy
asset utilisation
unit recovery
cost allocation drivers
```

The completed UI must label these clearly.

---

## 4.4 Show the current business mirror

Business Summary will show the current operating position.

It will answer:

```txt
What is the business doing now?
What margin does it generate?
What cost burden must that margin carry?
What is left after labour, assets, and overheads?
Is the current business position positive, negative, or under pressure?
```

The core chain is:

```txt
Revenue
- COGS
= Margin Pool

Margin Pool
- Labour
= Residual Margin after Labour

Residual Margin after Labour
- Assets
- Overheads
= Net Position
```

A negative result is not a system failure.

A bad business result is not the same as bad data.

---

## 4.5 Stress-test the first failure point: labour

Revenue Reality will test:

```txt
Does margin after COGS survive labour?
```

It isolates:

```txt
Revenue
- COGS
= Margin Pool

Margin Pool
- Labour
= Margin After Labour
```

This tells the user whether the business is already under pressure before assets and overheads are considered.

This will become one of the simplest and most powerful user-facing outputs:

```txt
Labour is covered by margin.
```

or:

```txt
Labour consumes more than the margin pool.
```

---

## 4.6 Stress-test productive assets

Asset Reality will test whether productive assets justify themselves at their entered utilisation.

It will answer:

```txt
Are productive assets recovering themselves?
What does each asset need to earn per hour?
Are assets underutilised?
Which asset creates the highest recovery pressure?
```

Example:

```txt
Digger annual cost
÷ Digger annual utilisation hours
= Required digger recovery rate
```

This prevents asset cost from being hidden inside general business cost.

---

## 4.7 Build the operating structure

Cost Allocation will define how the business is operationally organised.

The completed structure will be:

```txt
Division
↓
Operating Group
↓
Productive Labour Groups
Productive Assets
Automatic Overhead
```

Cost Allocation will answer:

```txt
Which divisions exist?
Which operating groups belong to each division?
Which labour groups are assigned?
Which productive assets are assigned?
How is overhead distributed?
Is any cost unassigned?
Has anything been over-assigned?
Is the structure valid enough to support recovery?
```

It will not build rates.

It will not make the final business decision.

It only defines the operating structure.

---

## 4.8 Test whether the structure recovers

Recovery Summary will automatically test whether the allocated structure can recover its cost burden.

It will answer:

```txt
Does this structure recover?
Which group is under-recovered?
What is the required recovery amount?
What is the recovery shortfall?
What recovery driver is being used?
```

Recovery Summary will be a zero-input page.

The user should not choose the recovery method there.

The recovery method is determined by the business mode and upstream structure.

Recovery Summary does not create sell rates.

It hands the recovery requirement to Rate Builder.

---

## 4.9 Build practical rates

Rate Builder will answer:

```txt
What do I need to charge?
```

It will build:

* labour rates
* machine rates
* machine + operator rates
* crew rates
* product/unit rates where valid
* minimum recoverable rates
* fully burdened cost rates
* suggested sell rates
* current rate comparison
* target rate comparison
* margin / markup options

Rate Builder converts the model into chargeable rates.

It does not create source costs.

It does not change the P&L.

It does not issue the final business verdict.

---

## 4.10 Diagnose the business outcome

Business Outcome will answer:

```txt
What is happening?
Why is it happening?
Where is the pressure coming from?
What needs to change?
```

It will classify pressure such as:

* revenue shortfall
* COG pressure
* labour pressure
* asset pressure
* overhead pressure
* recovery gap
* rate under-recovery
* mixed pressure
* source data blocked

Business Outcome is the diagnosis layer.

It converts technical recovery results into a plain-English business interpretation.

---

## 4.11 Model the selected fix

Business Modelling will sit between Business Outcome and Quote Checker.

The relationship is:

```txt
Business Outcome = this is the problem
Business Modelling = this is the selected fix
Quote Checker = does this quote follow the selected fix?
```

Business Modelling will allow the user to model:

* supplier cost changes
* category cost changes
* total COG changes
* labour productivity changes
* labour cost changes
* asset utilisation changes
* asset cost changes
* overhead changes
* selected rate changes
* all-rate changes
* margin target changes
* combined scenarios

It will create the selected target model and quote checker target set.

Quote Checker will only enforce selected targets.

No unselected scenario should flow silently into quote checking.

---

## 4.12 Check quotes before work starts

Quote Checker will enforce the selected model.

It will answer:

```txt
Does this quote align with the business model?
Does it recover minimum cost?
Does it meet the target margin?
Which lines are under-recovering?
Which lines miss the target rate?
Does the full quote pass or fail?
```

Quote Checker will compare quote lines against:

* approved minimum recoverable rates
* approved suggested sell rates
* selected Business Modelling targets
* selected margin targets
* selected markup targets
* material assumptions
* labour assumptions
* asset assumptions
* overhead assumptions

Quote Checker does not decide the model.

It checks whether the quote follows the approved model.

# 5. Practical Delivery Layer

The completed system must not stop at quote checking.

It must turn an approved quote into live operational control.

## 5.1 Approved quote to live job

Once a quote is accepted, Mirra should create a live job.

The live job should carry forward:

* job number
* job name
* client
* quote version
* approved quote value
* approved model version
* approved target set
* approved labour allowance
* approved material allowance
* approved asset allowance
* approved overhead recovery target
* Quote Checker result

The live job becomes the bridge between the model and actual delivery.

---

## 5.2 Works order capability

From the live job, Mirra should create works orders.

A works order should tell the business:

```txt
This is the job we priced.
This is the quote version we approved.
This is the model it must follow.
These are the hours allowed.
These are the material allowances.
These are the asset / machine assumptions.
These are the recovery targets.
```

The works order is the operational instruction.

It should be connected to:

* quote line
* SOQ line
* task
* labour allowance
* asset allowance
* material allowance
* recovery target

---

## 5.3 Purchase order capability

Mirra should create or track purchase orders against the job.

Purchase orders should link to:

* job
* quote line
* works order
* SOQ line
* supplier
* material category
* expected quantity
* expected unit cost
* expected total cost
* actual supplier invoice cost

This allows Mirra to compare:

```txt
Quoted allowance
vs
Purchase order commitment
vs
Supplier invoice
vs
Actual job result
```

Purchase orders become the control point between the quote and actual material/subcontractor cost.

---

## 5.4 Labour app / time tracking capability

Mirra should include a labour tracking app or time management layer.

This layer should record:

* live job
* staff member
* staff type
* work date
* start time
* finish time
* break
* task
* work category
* notes

Initial work categories should include:

* productive
* travel
* setup
* admin
* delay
* rework
* variation
* other

The labour app should compare:

```txt
Quoted labour hours allowed
vs
Actual productive labour hours
```

And:

```txt

vs

```

This will show whether a job failed because:

* the quote allowed too few hours
* the crew took too long
* non-productive time was too high
* rework occurred
* variation time was not recovered
* labour assumptions were wrong

---

## 5.5 Asset usage tracking

Where assets are material to recovery, Mirra should track asset usage against jobs.

Asset usage should connect:

* asset
* live job
* quote line or works order
* usage hours
* recovery rate
* recovered value
* actual cost basis

This will show whether productive assets recovered their required cost in real work.

---

## 5.6 Supplier invoices and actual costs

Supplier invoices should be matched to:

* job
* PO
* quote line
* SOQ line
* supplier
* material category
* cost type

The system should compare:

```txt
Expected PO cost
vs
Actual invoice cost
```

And:

```txt
Quoted allowance
vs
Actual supplier cost
```

This gives Mirra the evidence needed to identify supplier pressure, material pressure, cost overruns, and margin compression.

```txt

```

# 6. Return to Xero and P&L Loop

Once job actuals, supplier invoices, labour, asset usage, and invoicing are reconciled, the financial result should flow back to Xero.

Xero remains the accounting record.

Mirra remains the business model control system.

The intended relationship is:

```txt
Mirra checks and controls the model.
Xero records the financial truth.
```

The completed loop is:

```txt
Mirra checks the quote before work starts.
Mirra creates works orders and POs.
Mirra tracks labour, assets, supplier invoices, and job actuals.
Actual financial results are reconciled and recorded back into Xero.
Xero produces the monthly P&L.
Mirra imports the monthly P&L.
Mirra compares actual P&L performance back to the model.
Mirra creates model feedback.
The user approves or rejects model improvements.
Future quote targets are updated only after approval.
```

This is the recurring loop that validates the product.

* approve rate changes
* reject one-off issues
* defer review items


# 23. Final Principle

Mirra is a closed-loop recovery system.

It uses accounting truth to build the model.

It uses the model to set rates and quote targets.

It checks quotes before work starts.

It turns approved quotes into works orders, purchase orders, labour tracking, and job control.

It reconciles actual job performance back to Xero.

It uses the next monthly P&L to prove whether the model worked.

It creates feedback for controlled model improvement.

The current system is complex because the engine is being built.

The completed product must feel simple because the user should only see the answer first.

Final rule:

```txt
Build the complex engine.
Show the simple truth.
Let the user drill down only when they need proof.
```
