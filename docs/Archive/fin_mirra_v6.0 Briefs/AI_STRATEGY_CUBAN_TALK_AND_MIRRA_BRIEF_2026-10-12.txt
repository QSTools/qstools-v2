2. WHAT CUBAN SAID (condensed, in priority order for Mirra)
===============================================================================
 C1  The moat is unique IP, not software. Software sold as a standard product,
     "by the seat", is exposed because anyone can now describe it to an AI and
     get a version in minutes. What survives is proprietary data or logic the
     models cannot see or cheaply fetch. His example: DocuSign - signing law
     differs by city, state and country, and an agent crawling every
     jurisdiction would cost more in tokens than it is worth. A law firm told
     him its briefs are proprietary and client-specific, so no training on them.
     He is explicit that "a bit better, faster, cheaper" is not IP.

 C2  Do not publish; sell. Publishing or patenting makes the material available
     to every model to train on, which gives away the advantage. He tells
     hospitals and research groups this directly.

 C3  AI does not know the consequences of its actions. Hence a human buffer is
     needed who knows where, when and why to use it or not. His split: people
     who use AI so they do not have to learn anything, versus people who use it
     to learn everything. The second group gets the edge.

 C4  Rebuild around AI; do not bolt it on. Large companies spend heavily and see
     no return because they run the business the way they always did. The CEO
     has to be willing to reformulate the whole business. Starting fresh with AI
     is a completely different path from pre-AI.

 C5  Agents do the tedious work nobody gets to. Hung-over intern metaphor: an
     agent works 24/7 on the tasks sitting at the bottom of the to-do list.
     Best concrete example: Rebel Cheese built an agent that photographs each
     outgoing box, works out the size, checks the carrier price list, compares
     the carrier invoice, and files a credit request when they differ. Reported
     saving about $50,000 a month, fully automated.

 C6  Agents drift between model versions, so they need maintenance. He frames
     this as recurring income for anyone who sets them up for small and medium
     businesses (his figure: about $100/hour).

 C7  Iterate continuously. The tools change, staff and customers get smarter,
     so any threshold such as "10 employees, $1m sales" is not a place to stop.

 C8  Personalised teaching. AI can adapt questions to each student, keep the
     teacher and parent informed, and tell whether the student is engaged. Also
     NotebookLM turning a manual into a podcast.

 C9  Curiosity is the number one skill. He would not invest in someone who does
     not use AI to build a minimum viable product.

 C10 Be comfortable talking to AI at length; he gave a patent / bill of
     materials / business plan in 12 minutes as an example, noting it was not
     all correct but was a fast first pass.

 NOT RELEVANT TO MIRRA (recorded so nobody re-reads for them): foundation-model
 spending skepticism, world models, Sam Altman versus Dario Amodei, SaaS
 selloff commentary beyond C1, spam / slop in email, jobs outlook, NBA lightning

3. WHERE MIRRA ALREADY MATCHES
===============================================================================
 M1  Moat is the calculation chain, not the screens (C1).  [PK]
     The distinctive content is the verified mechanism and the judgment calls in
     it, for example: covered hours capped by labour (D1 settled walkthrough:
     asset hours 1,000, labour hours 900 -> 900 recoverable, group cost
     unchanged, cost per hour rises); overhead split between labour and assets
     by cost share rather than 100% on labour (BO-2 / F7); the non-productive
     cost policy; Best Case versus How the Business Runs. Also the BO / RB /
     smoke-test findings, each a case of something plausible being quietly
     wrong. A cold prompt to "build a cost allocation tool" will not reproduce
     these. The generic parts (import a P&L, store it, show a card) are not the
     moat.

 M2  Human stays the buffer (C3).  [PK for conventions, as supplied by owner]
     Apply-Patch with mandatory Patched / NOT FOUND / AMBIGUOUS confirmation,
     "a passing build does not confirm a patch", live-test on real data, separate
     confirmation for core calculation files, git status before commit. This is
     use-AI-to-learn, not use-AI-to-avoid-learning.

 M3  Built AI-natively (C4), for the dev process.  [PK for conventions]
     The workflow is not a traditional process with AI sprinkled in. Whether the
     PRODUCT is built the same way for its users is the open question in s4.

 M4  A clean, reconciled output contract with multiple consumers.  [PK]
     useBusinessOutcomeOutputContract.js feeds AI Export, Dashboard and Business
     Modelling. When F7 changed the overhead basis, the contract hook was
     updated so all consumers see the same basis. One source of truth, many
     consumers.
     CAUTION [INF]: the project notes show AI Export as a consumer of the
     contract. They do not, in what was read, describe what AI Export outputs
     or to whom. Do not assume it sends anything to an external model. Check.

 M5  Shows its working.  [PK]
     The calculation trace snapshots (calculation_trace_snapshot_*.json),
     CALCULATION_MAP and VARIABLE_TRACKER let a number be checked rather than
     trusted. This fits C3 (the buffer is built into the product) and is
     unusually strong for a tool of this kind.

 M6  Governing principle matches the agent idea.  [PK]
     Owner, D1/D4 brief: "the whole point of this tool is to reveal gaps - where
     the business is quietly losing money. If a gap is calculated but not shown,
     the tool is not keeping that promise." An agent extends "shown" from
     "visible if you look" to "pushed to you".

 M7  Some explanation of results.  [PK, partial]
     A health gauge explainer exists (build brief folded into Business Outcome;
     double-count check closed in Addendum 14, b5d1443). It explains a result;
     it is not known to adapt to who is reading. Weaker match to C8 than M1-M6.

4. WHAT MIRRA DOES NOT HAVE YET (the gaps relative to the talk)
===============================================================================
 G1  Agent for the end user (C5).
     Today Mirra answers when asked. The owner opens Business Outcome, reads the
     numbers, and spots a gap. Rebel Cheese's agent watches continuously and only
     interrupts a human when there is money on the table. Mirra has nothing that
     runs without a person loading a page.

     What already exists to build on [PK]:
       - Capacity coverage gap with a dollar value per working unit
         (capacity_coverage_gap[].gap_dollar_value; null with gap_type "none"
         when no linked group-level Rate Builder calculator, per tracker note
         2026-10-07).
       - unexplained_revenue (BO-11), idle cost inside group_cost_per_hour, and
         per-unit net profit figures.
       - The output contract, already shaped to leave the Business Outcome page.

     What does not exist [PK from searches for scheduling / notification terms
     returned nothing relevant; [INF] that nothing was missed]:
       (a) A TRIGGER. Calculations run client-side in React on render. Something
           would need to run the same chain headlessly on a schedule (a
           server-side job or timed serverless function) against stored data.
           Whether the hooks can run outside a browser is UNKNOWN [INF].
       (b) A COMPARISON. A single reading is not alertable; you need "new" or
           "worse than last time". That needs persisted history of calculated
           outputs. Mirra today stores user inputs and live-calculates; it has no
           historical snapshots of outputs. This is a first-time storage design.
       (c) DELIVERY. Email or in-app notification. None known.

     THE HARD PART IS THE RULE, NOT THE PLUMBING. What magnitude of gap, over
     what window, in which direction, deserves to interrupt an owner? Too
     sensitive and people learn to ignore it. NOT DEFINED. Candidate inputs
     already calculated: coverage gap dollar value, a unit tipping from viable
     to loss-making, non-productive cost share spike, unexplained_revenue.

 G2  Explanation adapted to the reader (C8). Same number and same words for an
     owner as for an accountant, as far as is known [INF]. Idea only.

 G3  Sold-as-a-service angle (C5/C6). Cuban's advice to young people is to set
     up agents for small businesses and keep them updated. Whether Mirra is
     something an adviser or consultant would use with clients is a business
     model question, not a feature. Parked.

ADDENDUM 1 - 2026-10-14

STATUS: [DISCUSSION / STRATEGY] - nothing built, nothing decided, no code touched.
SOURCE: Two further podcast transcripts pasted by the owner (Steve Eisman / Real
Eisman Playbook; Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein of Eon, on No Priors), plus a live
code-read verification this session (R1 and G1a below), plus owner-led strategy
discussion on a new benchmarking moat and its legal exposure.

A1. EON PODCAST - DATA AS THE ONLY DURABLE ASSET
    Core thesis: models and compute are "relatively ephemeral, almost zero
    switching cost." For any company - hotel chain, tech firm, doesn't matter -
    the most valuable thing it has is its data. Illustration: Google bought
    Spirit Airlines' data out of bankruptcy for ~$10M - not the planes - because
    real operational data (not synthetic) is scarce and hard to fake, and labs
    are short of genuine enterprise-shaped data to train and fine-tune agents on.
    Same podcast flagged a second-order risk worth parking, not acting on: AI
    agents as a new non-human security/compliance threat class inside an
    organisation - non-technical "shadow builders" creating agents that handle
    sensitive data without anyone tracking what they can see or do. Relevant to
    Mirra only once it holds a real database of client financial data (step 5
    below) - not before. No action now; revisit at that point.

A2. EISMAN PODCAST - THE MOAT TEST IS SWITCHING COST, NOT CLEVERNESS
    "This industry... has no moats." LLMs specifically: no loyalty, no
    switching cost - "if tomorrow another LLM shows up that's better than
    Claude, you'll switch." Contrast: Salesforce/ServiceNow-style software is
    "embedded in the companies that service them... hard to switch out of -
    that's a moat." Same test applies to Mirra: the product is only a moat if
    leaving it costs the client something real (their own calculated history),
    not because the calculation logic is clever.

A3. CLIENT-SIDE EXPOSURE - VERIFIED BY CODE READ, 2026-10-14 (upgrades G1a
    from [INF] to [PK, verified])
    Read directly, this session, with the owner's PC connected (read-only):
      - Every calculation hook (hooks/useBusinessOutcome.js,
        useBusinessOutcomeOutputContract.js, useAiBusinessState.js, and the
        rest of hooks/) is marked "use client" and imports directly from
        lib/calculations/*.js (calculateBusinessOutcome, etc.).
      - app/api/ contains exactly three routes: acc/search, demo-login,
        pnl-import. None of them touch the calculation chain.
      - CONFIRMED: the entire calculation chain (40+ files in
        lib/calculations/ - Business Outcome, Cost Summary, Labour, Rate
        Builder, Asset, all of it) ships into the browser bundle today. The
        R2/C1 moat concern from the main brief (the proprietary logic is the
        valuable part, not the screens) is therefore a live exposure, not a
        hypothetical one. This is the concrete trigger for the server
        migration decision at A5 below.

A4. AI EXPORT - VERIFIED BY CODE READ, 2026-10-14 (resolves D-E)
    hooks/useAiBusinessState.js composes existing output contracts (Business
    Summary, Business Modelling, Model Readiness, Business Outcome) via a pure
    selector, buildAiBusinessStateExport. The only consumer is
    app/ai-business-state/page.jsx, which JSON.stringify's the object into a
    "JSON export preview" <pre> block - a read-only preview page, nothing
    more. package.json has no OpenAI/Anthropic SDK and no third-party AI
    dependency at all. CONFIRMED: nothing in Mirra currently sends reconciled
    figures or logic to any external model. "AI Export" is scaffolding for a
    future downstream consumer, not a live pipe anywhere today. R1 is resolved
    as a non-issue for now - revisit this finding specifically if AI Export is
    ever wired to an actual third-party model call.

A5. SERVER-SIDE MIGRATION DECISION (follows directly from A3)
    Decided: migrate the calculation/selector files to run server-side, whole
    files only, never split mid-logic (splitting risks silently changing
    behaviour partway through a calculation chain that depends on exact
    order). Entry-point design: a thin dispatcher/registry
    (app/api/calculate/route.js + a lookup table), not one API route per
    calculation - the dispatcher does no calculation logic itself and stays
    inside the existing ~600-line file convention. This buys time before a
    copier can even build a working clone, on top of whatever data-moat
    advantage exists once step 5 (the database) is live.

A6. UNITS-BEFORE-LAUNCH DECISION
    Decided: build units (not just hours-based working units) through the
    tool fully before launch, not as a coming-soon/gated feature. Reasoning:
    units will likely require changes to shared calculation files; making
    those changes AFTER a client database of live financial history exists
    risks corrupting or tangling both live numbers and stored history at once
    (owner's own prior experience: wiping messy personal databases in Excel/
    Power BI/SQLite was recoverable there - it is not an acceptable risk with
    real client financial data in play). Accepted this likely adds ~6 months
    to the timeline; judged worth it to avoid building the database against a
    calculation chain that is still structurally changing.

A7. THE THIRD MOAT - ANONYMISED CROSS-CLIENT BENCHMARKING
    Trigger: in construction right now (the owner's own trade), many
    businesses are pricing below true cost to retain staff through a downturn,
    and there is currently no way to tell who is making a smart bet versus who
    is slowly failing - the market is "unlevel" because nobody can see real
    comparative data. Historical analogue raised and rejected as insufficient:
    Rawlinson's rate guide (NZ), now barely available, static and
    price-only. Mirra's version would be alive (updated monthly) and would show
    cost-structure truth (true cost coverage), not just quoted price.

    Three layers (only the first exists in the current build order):
      1. A business's own modelled-vs-actual history over time - this is the
         switching-cost moat, already step 5 below.
      2. Cross-business snapshot - where a business sits vs anonymised peers
         right now.
      3. Market trend over time - aggregated median rate/cost-structure per
         category, tracked monthly, showing real market drift (e.g. prices
         softening in a downturn) vs single-point noise.
    Layers 2 and 3 are the first genuine network effect identified anywhere in
    this strategy work - everything else discussed (including A2's switching-
    cost moat) is per-client and does not get stronger as other clients join.
    Switching cost (layer 1) is small-but-certain and available from day one;
    the benchmark moat (layers 2-3) is larger-upside but conditional on client
    volume, time and consent, and is explicitly sequenced AFTER layer 1 is
    working, not parallel to it.

A8. WHAT THE BENCHMARK LAYER NEEDS - GAP ANALYSIS AGAINST THE EXISTING CHAIN
    Already exists, verified via project knowledge, no new calculation logic
    required: Business Outcome per_source (achieved rate, true cost, gap per
    working unit per group); capacity_coverage_gap (units running below cost);
    Labour Recovery overhead_per_hour / true_cost_per_hour per staff type;
    Recovery Summary stream shares (already percentage-based, comparable
    across business sizes); Cost Summary required_recovery_rate; revenue (for
    a size band) already in the P&L output contract; industry/trade - owner
    confirmed this is already captured somewhere in the tool, not a gap.

    Genuinely new, small, outside the existing calculation chain:
      1. A stored consent flag (benchmark_data_sharing_consent + timestamp),
         captured plainly at signup, scoped narrower than a typical AI-training
         opt-in - anonymised ratios only, never raw data, never model training.
      2. A size-band bucketing calculation - trivial, derived from the
         existing revenue field.
      3. An aggregation module - the one genuinely new piece of logic.
         Queries all consenting businesses' stored ratios grouped by
         category + size band, computes median/range per group. This is a
         new, self-contained module sitting OUTSIDE the existing calculation
         chain, reading only finished output-contract data, writing to its
         own storage. It does not modify or depend on the internals of
         Business Outcome / Cost Summary / etc. Functionally "an M-code-style
         query of the database" once the database exists - the complexity is
         in trustworthiness (consent honoured, category reliable, sample-size
         floor met), not in the query logic itself.
    HARD DEPENDENCY: the aggregation module cannot be built or tested until
    the database (step 5) exists and holds real, persistent, queryable
    cross-client rows. Sequenced immediately after step 5, not parallel to it
    and not earlier (see the build-order brief for the numbered sequence).

A9. SAMPLE-SIZE AND GO-TO-MARKET IMPLICATION
    Research (web search, this conversation): a practical floor for even
    directional benchmarking signal is roughly 20-30 businesses per
    category x size-band combination; a rigorous figure is 200-385. ATO small
    business benchmarks (AU) are a real-world analogue, segmented by industry
    and turnover band. Implication: go deep in one trade (construction, the
    owner's own background - "the tool is pretty well built around
    construction" already) rather than spread thin across many trades in
    year one, since depth reaches a usable sample threshold faster than
    breadth does. Likely generalises later to other trades (window cleaners,
    mechanics, etc.) but that is explicitly a later decision, not a now one.

A10. VALUATION RESEARCH
    Data-moat companies: industry research (Opagio and others) puts these at
    roughly 5-10x revenue valuation multiples, versus 2-5x for an otherwise
    comparable SaaS product without one. Typical private SaaS multiples run
    2.5x-10x ARR depending on scale, quality and durability. The data/moat
    value is almost never its own line item in a sale - it shows up as a
    higher multiple on the whole revenue figure, because it is what makes
    growth and retention defensible (low churn, a client base that is hard to
    poach, plus an embedded option on the benchmark feature a buyer could
    build on day two). This directly answers the owner's original question
    from the main brief - "how do I stay ahead if someone copies my product":
    you do not prevent copying (accepted as inevitable), you make copying
    insufficient, because the client history and eventual benchmark pool
    cannot be copied, only built over time, and a copier starts that clock at
    zero.

A11. PRICE-SIGNALING LEGAL RISK - FLAGGED BY THE OWNER, RESEARCHED THIS SESSION
    The owner raised this unprompted and correctly: a cross-client
    benchmarking feature sits adjacent to real antitrust precedent, not a
    theoretical concern.
      - RealPage (DOJ, ongoing litigation/settlement): competitors fed data
        into one platform, which aggregated it and generated pricing
        recommendations landlords adopted at high rates - "using software as
        the sharing mechanism does not immunize this scheme." Key aggravating
        facts: current/near-real-time data, and the tool's output functioned
        as a de facto pricing recommendation.
      - Agri Stats (DOJ + State AGs settlement): weekly, facility-level,
        non-public price/cost/output data shared between meat processors -
        "particularly problematic was providing non-public price and sales
        data at a facility level on a weekly basis."
      - Favourable precedent: Ninth Circuit, Gibson v. Cendyn Group - merely
        subscribing to a shared pricing tool, even knowing competitors also
        subscribe, does NOT by itself violate Sherman Act Section 1. What
        distinguished RealPage was commingled live data AND delegated pricing
        authority, not the mere existence of a benchmark.
      - Legitimate-benchmarking guidance (FindLaw): "sterilize" raw data by
        aggregating or masking it so it cannot be tied to one company; limit
        the exchange to objective, historical information.
      - Landmine specifically flagged: marketing language implying the tool
        sets or aligns prices ("everybody is effectively pricing at the market
        rate") has been used as direct evidence of intent in at least one
        class action. Mirra's public framing of this feature must never imply
        a recommended rate or "the market rate to charge."
    DECIDED (design direction, not yet built, not yet lawyer-reviewed): show
    a BAND (e.g. "businesses your size in this trade typically run 55-65%
    gross margin"), not a specific number; historical/monthly, never live;
    combined with the sample-size floor at A9; the tool states where a
    business sits against the band and stops there - it never suggests a
    rate or action. This combination is judged the most defensible design
    achievable, but it is a direction, not a cleared design.
    REQUIRED BEFORE THIS FEATURE IS BUILT: a lawyer reviews the actual design
    (band width, sample floor, update cadence, exact UI copy) and signs off.
    Not optional, not a "build first, ask later" item - flagged as a hard
    gate in the build-order brief (see A12).
