D1  HOURS GUARDRAIL IN COST ALLOCATION (replaces/extends OPEN-A)
  Problem: assets are entered on their own, each with standalone hours.
  Cost Allocation takes the MAX of a group's asset hours, which is right when
  one operator watches several machines at the same time, and understates the
  labour needed when the operator does them one after another. Nothing
  catches a case such as two machines entered at 100 hrs/month each when the
  operator really gives 50 to each. The two situations give identical numbers.
  Owner decisions:
    - The check lives in Cost Allocation only. Not in Assets (an asset does
      not know its group) and not in Rate Builder (hours are already fixed
      upstream by then).
    - If the hours look wrong, the message should send the user back to the
      Assets module to correct the hours.
    - A hard "one asset per group" rule is rejected: a factory operator
      legitimately runs two or three machines at once.
    - Splitting sequential work into separate groups (Paul: one group for
      Test Asset, one for Pump) needs NO code change - it is a data setup
      choice for the test dataset.
  Existing behaviour to build on: labour_coverage_warning fires when labour
  hours are SHORT of asset hours (confirmed: 26 hr gap at E.1), not when
  labour is in surplus. Surplus side was parked in RB1 brief s5f because it
  needs to know if labour is machine-bound. Option under consideration (NOT
  decided): one per-group "confirmed as run together" flag that acknowledges
  the shortfall warning and also records intent for the surplus-side warning.
  Settled by walkthrough: with asset hours 1,000 and labour hours 900, the
  group is capped to 900 recoverable hours, total group cost is unchanged,
  and the cost per hour rises. Owner agrees this is correct: the asset cannot
  run without its labour.

D2  COST ALLOCATION SHOULD NOT SHOW FINAL NUMBERS
  Owner: Cost Allocation is for assigning costs. Group totals and rates were
  shown only to build and verify. Put them behind a reveal; Business Outcome
  (and possibly Rate Builder) is where final figures are presented. No change
  to any calculation - display only. Touches every group card.

D3  BUSINESS SUMMARY - IS THE SCREEN STILL NEEDED?
  Owner's gut feel: it is about existing revenue and may not need displaying.
  Findings from the project notes:
    - The calculation half (recovery_hours_used, required_recovery_per_driver
      and related fields) is spread into the Recovery Summary input and MUST
      stay. Hours mode no longer displays those fields.
    - The visible half (Open-Hour Reality card, P&L bridge) is a macro

      Outcome, so Business Outcome does not replace it.
    - Open question: keep it as its own screen, fold the bridge into Business
      Outcome, or hide the screen and let it run in the background.

ADDENDUM 2 - SIMPLE VERSION, SAME SESSION (2026-10-04)
===============================================================================

D1 RESTATED SIMPLY: two machines, not enough labour hours to run them both.
  Right now the system quietly knows but says nothing. Needed, as one job:
    - teach it the difference between one person running two machines at
      once, vs. one person running them one after another (the checkbox)
    - actually show the warning on screen when hours don't add up, pointing
      the user back to Assets to fix it

D4 NEW - too MUCH labour for the asset (the mirror case). Real example from
  the owner: a civil contractor let go digger drivers who refused to do
  anything but drive the digger. Work is quiet, so the digger sits idle and
  that labour is now a genuine loss, not spare capacity doing something
  useful elsewhere.
  Needed: a way to mark a labour assignment as tied to one asset only (won't
  flex to other work). When marked this way, that person's idle hours are a
  real cost gap, not just surplus - the system should reveal it, not stay
  silent.

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE (owner, stated plainly): the whole point of this tool is
  to reveal gaps - where the business is quietly losing money. If a gap is
  calculated but not shown, the tool is not keeping that promise. Applies to
  D1, D4, and the existing labour_coverage_warning (C22) alike.
