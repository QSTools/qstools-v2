# 4. All productive labour weighted rate

## Locked behaviour

**All productive labour weighted rate is read-only.**

It is not an editable labour rate input.

It is calculated from the saved staff-type charge-out rates, weighted by productive hours.

The calculation logic is:

* staff-type charge-out rate multiplied by that staff type’s productive hours
* total modelled productive labour revenue divided by total productive labour hours

This produces the weighted current charge-out rate.

Current confirmed example:

* Junior / Trainee: saved and included
* Operator / Delivery Staff: saved and included
* Senior Operator / Senior Staff: saved and included
* Site Crew: saved and included
* all-productive weighted rate now shows **$70/hr**
* no staff type rates are missing

# 6. Calculator model decision

## Current state

The UI still shows:

* Saved calculator
* Calculator name
* New rate
* Duplicate
* Delete

## Agreed principle

The user has questioned whether this calculator model is necessary.

The agreed view is:

* multiple calculator types are probably not needed at this stage
* the system should not imply multiple competing ways of calculating labour rates
* there should be one correct calculation method
* different labour sources use the same calculation logic

## Current action

The calculator UI has **not yet been removed**.

It remains in place for now because the immediate priority was to fix staff-type rate saving without disrupting the page.

## Future decision

A future cleanup can simplify this area to remove or hide:

* saved calculator dropdown
* calculator name
* duplicate/delete controls

Unless a future reason is found to support deliberate pricing scenarios.

# 7. Minimum recoverable labour rate

## Locked concept

The minimum recoverable labour rate shown in the Labour Rates Builder includes:

* true productive labour cost
* allocated business overhead recovery

It does **not** add non-productive labour directly into the charge-out rate.

Non-productive labour is shown as business recovery pressure, not loaded directly into the minimum recoverable hourly rate.

This preserves the distinction between:

* productive labour cost recovery
* business recovery pressure
* full business outcome reconciliation

# 9. P&L implied labour benchmark card

## Locked decision

The P&L implied labour recovery card should **not** appear inside the Labour Rates Builder.

Reason:

The P&L cannot cleanly prove labour recovery on its own because revenue, COGS, labour, assets, materials, overhead, and margin are blended together.

Showing a P&L implied labour rate inside Rates Builder implies too much accuracy too early.

## Current action

The P&L implied benchmark card has been removed from the Labour Rates Builder UI.

The underlying logic may still exist in the system for later use.

## Future location

This logic belongs later in:

* Business Outcome
* Recovery Outcome
* final P&L reconciliation layer

It should not drive or confuse the Labour Rates Builder.

---

# 10. Source-of-truth rule

## Locked rule

Each calculation must have one owner.

For this area:

* Labour module owns labour cost truth
* Recovery / Cost Allocation should own overhead split truth
* Rates Builder consumes the result and compares assumed charge-out rates
* Business Outcome / Recovery Outcome later reconciles actual P&L truth

Rates Builder should not become a business outcome page.

---

# 11. Overhead allocation issue still to audit

The next major audit is overhead allocation.

The question is:

**Has overhead distribution already been done inside Cost Allocation, and does it match what Rates Builder is currently using?**

This still needs to be checked.

## Locked architectural direction

Overhead distribution should ultimately happen in the allocation/recovery layer, not inside Labour or Assets independently.

The desired structure is:

* Cost Allocation / Recovery layer decides the split
* Labour consumes its allocated overhead share
* Assets consume their allocated overhead share
* Rates Builder displays the minimum recoverable rate based on those outputs

---

# 12. Proportional overhead allocation decision

The user confirmed that proportional allocation is the preferred logic.

Overhead should not be split equally across staff types.

Instead, it should follow the weighted labour structure.

Example:

* site crew cost base: 40
* foreman cost base: 60
* total cost base: 100

Then:

* site crew carries 40%
* foreman carries 60%

This is the preferred logic because higher-cost labour should carry proportionally more recovery burden than lower-cost labour.

---

# 13. Productive asset running cost correction

A key unresolved issue remains:

When allocating overhead to labour, productive asset-owned costs must not be included in the labour overhead pool.

Examples:

* productive asset fuel
* maintenance
* running costs
* registration
* other asset-owned operating costs

These should be recovered through Assets, not Labour.

The corrected distributable overhead pool should be:

**general overhead pool minus productive asset-owned running costs**

This prevents Labour and Assets from both recovering the same cost.

# 16. Current implementation outcome

The Labour Rates Builder now achieves the intended purpose:

## It answers:

* What do we currently charge for each labour source?
* What is the weighted all-productive labour rate?
* What is the minimum recoverable labour rate?
* Are we above or below the required rate?
* What is the surplus or leak per hour?
* What is the margin after full recovery?

## It does not answer:

* whether the full business is profitable
* whether material margin is cross-subsidising labour
* whether assets are under-recovered
* whether the P&L proves the assumptions
* whether the final business outcome is acceptable

Those belong later in Business Outcome / Recovery Outcome.
